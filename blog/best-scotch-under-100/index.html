How We Picked the Best Scotch for the Money

People who search for the best scotch whisky under $100, or simply the best scotch for the money, want value, and value has four parts you can check on the label: age, strength, region and price. By law, Scotch must be matured in oak casks for at least three years and bottled at no less than 40% alcohol by volume, according to the Scotch Whisky Association, so every bottle here clears that floor. What separates them is how far above the floor they go.

We used four simple tests:

  • A real age statement where possible, because it tells you how long the whisky waited. Of the 111 bottles under $100, 60 carry one.
  • Strength above the minimum. Bottles at 43% to 46% usually taste fuller than 40% bottles, and a few go further. Aberlour A’bunadh is 61.2%.
  • A clear style. Each pick shows one recognisable style, so you learn something about your own taste.
  • Price per year of age. Dividing price by age gives a quick view of value, which we use in the table below.

We left out bottles where we could not state a verified style, and we describe flavour only where the producer publishes it. That keeps this a buying guide, not a list of invented tasting notes. If you want to learn how to read what is printed on a label before you buy, First Phil Whisky’s How to Read a Whisky Label video is an independent explainer worth a few minutes.

Best Sherry Cask Scotch Under $100

Sherry-cask maturation gives Scotch a darker, richer style, and it is easy to buy under $100. GlenDronach says its 12 year old is matured in the finest Pedro Ximénez and Oloroso sherry casks, and that the distillery has crafted single malts since 1826 in the Valley of Forgue in the Highlands, according to the official GlenDronach site. The GlenDronach 12 Year is $44.99 and 43% ABV, which makes it one of the best sherried bargains in the range.

Our sherry-led picks under $100:

  • GlenDronach 12 Year, $44.99, 43%. Pedro Ximénez and Oloroso casks, per the distillery.
  • Aberlour 12 Year Double Cask, $44.99, 40%. A gentler, more balanced entry to the style.
  • Glenfarclas 10 Year, $44.99, 40%, and the Glenfarclas 15 Year at $69.99 and 46%, from a family-run Speyside distillery known for sherry maturation.
  • Macallan 12 Double Cask, $74.99, 40%. The distillery describes its Double Cask range as sherry seasoned American and European oak.
  • Aberlour A’bunadh Cask Strength, $69.99, 61.2%. The strongest bottle in this guide, best with a few drops of water.

If you want one bottle to try first, the best single malt scotch under 100 for most people is the GlenDronach 12 for sherry or the Glenfiddich 12 for an easy start. For a sherry bottle, the best sherry cask scotch under $100 to try first is the GlenDronach 12 for value, and the Macallan 12 Double Cask is the better-known name. Best Non-Peated Scotch Under $100

If you do not like smoke, the good news is that most of the best value in this range is unpeated or lightly peated. Look at the Speyside and Highland bottles above: Glenfiddich 12, Glenmorangie Original 10, Aberlour 12 Double Cask, GlenDronach 12, Singleton and Macallan 12 are all from regions where peat is not the headline style. Our regional labels in the catalog make this easy to filter, and the Speyside and Highland groups hold the largest number of bottles.

For a non-peated pick by occasion:

  • Everyday sipping: Glenfiddich 12 Year at $39.99.
  • A fuller feel without smoke: Glenmorangie Quinta Ruban 14 Year at $64.99 and 46%, or Tomatin 18 at $79.99.
  • A sherried gift: Macallan 12 Double Cask at $74.99, or GlenDronach 15 Year at $74.99 and 46%.

All of these are single malts, which means each comes from one distillery.

Good Inexpensive Scotch: Bottles Under $50

Fifty-four Scotches in our range cost $50 or less, so you can find good inexpensive Scotch without going near the $100 limit. Useful options include:

  • Blends with a 12 year age statement: Dewar’s 12 Year and Chivas Regal 12 Year at $29.99, and Johnnie Walker Black Label 12 Year at $34.99.
  • Entry single malts: Glen Moray Classic at $29.99, and Speyburn 10 Year and Glenmorangie Original 10 Year at $34.99.
  • A blended malt: Monkey Shoulder at $29.99, which our catalog lists as blended malt Scotch.

For the best cheap scotch whiskey for mixing, a blend is the sensible choice. A blend or a young single malt is a sensible choice for highballs and mixed drinks, while a 10 to 12 year old single malt is better for sipping. At this price the main risk is buying on a famous name, so check the age and strength first.

How to Choose

Work through these steps in order and you will land on the right bottle:

  1. Decide how you will drink it. Neat favours single malts, and mixers favour blends.
  2. Choose a style. Smoky, sherried or light and fruity.
  3. Set a ceiling. Under $50 buys a good everyday bottle, and $50 to $100 buys an age statement of 15 to 18 years.
  4. Check the strength. Bottles at 43% or more feel fuller.
  5. Buy two. Our minimum order is $249.99, so two or three bottles from different styles, such as a Speyside, an Islay and a sherried malt, clears it and teaches you your taste.
