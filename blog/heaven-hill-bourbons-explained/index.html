Heaven Hill 90th Anniversary Explained: The 9 Year Bourbon, Grain to Glass and the Bottles You Can Buy

By Caskworth Editorial Published September 6, 2026Updated November 9, 20269 min read

The Heaven Hill 90th Anniversary bourbon is a limited release that the distillery lists as a 9 year old Kentucky straight bourbon at 107 proof (53.5% ABV), made from 204 barrels distilled on 13 December 2015, with a suggested price of $124.99. We do not currently list it, so this guide gives the official facts, explains the distillery’s Grain to Glass line and what bottled in bond means, and compares six Heaven Hill bottles we do stock, from $17.99 to $499.99, with price per pour.

The Heaven Hill 90th Anniversary Bourbon

Heaven Hill’s own 90th Anniversary release page gives the specification. The bourbon is listed as 9 years old, although the barrels matured nine years and eight months in Rickhouses G and H, on the fourth and sixth floors. It is 107 proof and non-chill filtered, from 204 barrels. The page calls 13 December 2015 the produced date, which marked the distillery’s 80th anniversary, and says the mash bill is 78% corn, 12% malted barley and 10% rye.

The blend is 70% Kentucky straight bourbon and 30% Kentucky straight bourbon aged in heavy char barrels, with a deep alligator char layer. Tasting notes on the page are caramel, vanilla, toasted spice and oak warmth. The suggested retail price is $124.99, and the page says it is available nationwide in limited quantities from October 2025, although the press release is dated 4 November 2025, so we describe it simply as a late 2025 release.

For a spoken view, The Fysh Tank’s Heaven Hill 90th Anniversary Bourbon | Worth The Hype? is a useful Heaven Hill 90th Anniversary review, and a Heaven Hill 90th anniversary msrp of $124.99 gives you a yardstick for any secondary-market price. Remember that a single review is one view, and that we have not poured the bottle ourselves.

Heaven Hill Grain to Glass

Heaven Hill announced Heaven Hill Grain to Glass on 19 June 2024. It has three expressions, each with its own mash bill: a Kentucky straight bourbon at 52% corn, 35% rye and 13% malted barley, a wheated bourbon at 52% corn, 35% wheat and 13% malted barley, and a rye at 63% rye, 24% corn and 13% malted barley. All are aged over six years at the Cox’s Creek rickhouse site and are non-chill filtered, in 700 ml bottles.

The proofs are 107 for the bourbon, 121 for the wheated bourbon, and 123.2 for the rye, which the page calls the only barrel proof expression of the series. The distillery says the master distiller selects a corn seed varietal each year with Beck’s Hybrids, and that Peterson Farms grows it on one of two Nelson County sites, one across the street from the Bardstown facility. The page does not state a price or distribution.

That detail is worth knowing because some retailers describe the grain as grown on the distillery’s own estate, which the official page does not say. If you are comparing a Grain to Glass review with the Heaven Hill Grain to Glass bourbon, wheated and rye bottles on shelves, check the label for the corn varietal, mash bill and release year, because they change each edition. A wheated option is covered more broadly in our best wheated bourbon under 100 guide.

What Heaven Hill Bottled in Bond Means

Heaven Hill bottled in bond means a bottle that meets the federal rule in 27 CFR 5.88: one distilling season, one distiller, one distillery, at least four years in wood and 100 proof, which is 50% ABV. The label must name the distillery. Our bottled in bond guide sets out the rule and the history in full, and the short version for buyers is that the label tells you the age floor, the strength and who made it.

Our catalogue lists the Heaven Hill Bottled-in-Bond 7 Year at $45.00 and 50% ABV. This is the bottle people mean when they search for a Heaven Hill 7 year or a Heaven Hill bottled in bond 7 year, and a fair Heaven Hill bottled in bond review would note that the strength is exactly the bonded 50%, the age statement is seven years, and the price per pour is about $1.80. We have not poured it for this guide, so we do not quote tasting notes.

If you want to learn what the bonded label does for flavour, taste it beside a 43% everyday bourbon from the same distillery, and write down what the extra strength and age change.

Evan Williams Bourbon and the Everyday Bottles

Wikipedia’s Heaven Hill article names Evan Williams and Elijah Craig as the flagship bourbon labels, with Larceny as a wheated bourbon, and Henry McKenna and Parker’s Heritage Collection among the others. Anyone looking for an Evan Williams bourbon will find two bottles in our catalogue: Evan Williams Black Label at $17.99 and 43%, and Evan Williams Single Barrel Vintage at $29.99 and 43.3%, which our catalogue lists with a 10 year age statement.

The Larceny Small Batch at $29.99 and 46% is the wheated option, and our catalogue also lists a Larceny Barrel Proof at $49.99 and 61%. Anyone writing a Heaven Hill bourbon review of the everyday range would start with Black Label, because at $17.99 it is the cheapest bottle here. Elijah Craig, the other flagship, is a separate bottle with its own range.

A Heaven Hill Tasting Flight

The cheapest way to understand the distillery is a three-glass flight. Pour half an ounce each of Evan Williams Black Label at 43%, Larceny Small Batch at 46% and Heaven Hill Bottled-in-Bond 7 Year at 50%, which together cost $92.98. Taste them from the lowest strength to the highest, so that the strongest does not overwhelm the others, and keep a glass of water nearby.

Nose each glass gently before sipping, because a hard sniff of 50% spirit mostly registers alcohol. Take a small first sip, hold it on the tongue, and note whether you taste sweetness, spice or oak first. Add a few drops of water to the bonded bottle and taste it again. Write one line per glass.

Most drinkers find that the wheated Larceny is the softest, that the Black Label is the lightest, and that the bonded 7 Year has the most body. That is a useful lesson in how strength, grain and age shape a bourbon, and it costs far less than a single limited release. Keep the notes with the bottles, and revisit them after a month to see whether your first impression holds.

Visiting Heaven Hill

Wikipedia says Heaven Hill is family owned and headquartered in Bardstown, Kentucky, and that its Bourbon Heritage Center opened in 2004 on the old distillery grounds with exhibits and tours. The brand’s own visitor experience is sold as the Heaven Hill Bourbon Experience, and tour options and booking change, so check the official site before you plan a trip. People who search for a Heaven Hill tour or a Heaven Hill bourbon experience are looking for that visitor offer, not for a bottle.

If you cannot visit, the cheapest way to get a feel for the distillery is to taste two or three of the bottles above side by side, starting with the Black Label and finishing with the bonded 7 Year.
