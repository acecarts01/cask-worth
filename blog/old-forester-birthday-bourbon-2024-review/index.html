What Birthday Bourbon Is

Old Forester Birthday Bourbon is an annual limited release that honours the founder’s birthday. Wikipedia’s Old Forester article says George Garvin Brown, the founder of Brown-Forman, first bottled and marketed Old Forester in 1870, and that it was the first bourbon sold exclusively in sealed bottles, which guarded against adulteration. It also says Birthday Bourbon has been released every year since 2002 through a national sweepstakes, with winners chosen on 2 September, Brown’s birthday.

The Bourbon Culture’s release-by-year page adds that each annual selection is drawn from barrels distilled on the same day of production, which is why people use the term vintage dated bourbon for it. Anyone searching for a vintage dated bourbon collector bottle should note that this is the sense of the phrase: one production day, not a blend across many.

Old Forester’s own site says the release celebrates the founder on his birthday, and that recent bottles have been available through sweepstakes, with later sales at the distillery on random days. That is worth knowing: the normal route to this bottle is a sweepstakes or the secondary market, which is why availability and price vary. For the wider picture, our allocated bourbon guide explains why bottles like this are hard to find.

Old Forester Birthday Bourbon Review: The 2024 Release

According to Spirited Drinks, Paste Magazine and The Bourbon Culture, the 2024 release is 12 years old and bottled at 107 proof, or 53.5% ABV. The Bourbon Culture lists 209 barrels, aged in warehouses L and G. The same pages note that 107 proof sets a new high for the series, beating the 105 proof of the 2019 release, which the Bourbon Culture lists as 11 years old from 120 barrels. We could not find the barrel fill date for 2024 from any source we trust, so we do not state one.

Paste’s review describes the nose as vanilla buttercream, honey toffee, fresh apple, clove, charred oak and dried tobacco, and the palate as molasses spice cake, cola spice, roasty espresso, tobacco, pepper and black cherry, with a sweet char on the finish. Those are one reviewer’s notes. We have not poured the bottle ourselves, and each year’s release differs, so treat them as a guide.

For a spoken take, Barrel Man Drinks’ 2024 Old Forester Birthday Bourbon Review is a useful companion.

The Strength Discrepancy

Our catalogue lists Old Forester Birthday Bourbon 2024 at 49% ABV, which is 98 proof. The sources above give 107 proof, or 53.5%. Those two figures do not match, and a 4.5 point difference changes how the whiskey tastes and how much water it needs. We cannot tell from here which is correct for the bottle in stock, so check the strength on the label and ask before you buy.

The same caution applies to the Old Forester 1920 Prohibition Style, which our catalogue lists at 45%. Wikipedia lists the 1920 at 115 proof, or 57.5%, so that entry also needs confirming. We would rather flag the conflict than quote a number we cannot support.

At 53.5% the Birthday Bourbon would be a high-proof pour, so expect more heat than a standard 45% bottle and add a few drops of water if it is too hot. If the label says otherwise, trust the label.

Birthday Bourbon Beside the Everyday Bottles

The Old Forester Birthday Bourbon 2024 at $249.99 is the top of the Old Forester bottles we list. Two lower-priced bottles show the range. The Old Forester 1920 Prohibition Style at $59.99 belongs to the Whiskey Row series, and our catalogue notes that only the Birthday Bourbon is currently carried from that series. The Old Forester Rye at $28.00 is the everyday option.

Wikipedia lists the core range as Classic at 86 proof, Signature at 100 proof and 1920 Prohibition Style at 115 proof, announced in 2016. That makes the 107 proof of the 2024 Birthday Bourbon higher than the Signature and lower than the 1920, which is a useful way to picture its strength.

If you want to try the brand before spending $249.99, the rye and the 1920 are the sensible starting points, because they show the house style at a fraction of the cost. For another limited release, our Sazerac 18 Year review covers an aged rye from the Buffalo Trace Antique Collection.

How 2024 Compares With Earlier Years

The two years for which we found consistent published details give a useful comparison. The Bourbon Culture lists the 2019 release as 11 years old, bottled at 105 proof from 120 barrels in Warehouse I, with a barrel fill date of 15 May 2008. It lists the 2024 release as 12 years old, bottled at 107 proof from 209 barrels in warehouses L and G. So the 2024 bottle is a year older, two proof points higher and drawn from a larger group of barrels.

More barrels can mean a larger release, although we do not have a bottle count to confirm that. Each year’s selection still comes from barrels distilled on a single day of production, so the differences from year to year come from the day chosen, the warehouse and the years of ageing. That is why collectors compare vintages, and why a review of one year is not a promise about another.

If you own more than one year, the best way to learn the difference is to pour them side by side, using the same glass and the same amount of water. Record the proof, the age and your notes in one place. Over time, that record becomes more useful than any published score.

How to Taste a High-Proof Birthday Bourbon

A bottle at this strength rewards patience. Pour about half an ounce into a tulip glass and let it rest for several minutes, because high-proof bourbon needs time to settle. Nose it gently, first from a distance and then closer, and look for the vanilla, toffee, apple and clove that the published review mentions. A hard sniff of 53.5% spirit mostly registers alcohol, so do not rush it.

Take a very small first sip and hold it on the tongue. The first taste is mostly heat, so do not judge on it. Add two or three drops of water, wait a minute and taste again. Many drinkers find the flavours of spice cake, cola and black cherry open up once the strength is softened. Stop adding water before the whiskey turns thin.

Serve it at room temperature, with a glass of water nearby, and avoid strong food before or during the tasting. A good companion is a lower-proof Old Forester, such as the rye, poured the same way, because it shows how the extra age and strength change the character. Write a few lines about each, then keep the notes with the bottle.

Finally, store the bottle upright, away from heat and light, and reseal it after each pour. An opened bottle changes slowly with air, so finish it within a sensible time if flavour matters more to you than display.
