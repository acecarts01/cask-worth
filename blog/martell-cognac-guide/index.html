What Is Martell Cognac? The 1715 House

Wikipedia's Martell article says the house was founded in 1715 by Jean Martell, a merchant born on Jersey in 1694, who started a trading business in Cognac on the Charente river. It says Seagram took control in 1987 and that Pernod Ricard acquired Martell after Seagram's break-up in 2001, although another list in the article gives different dates, so we do not rely on a single year. It reports around 14 million bottles a year by 2015, making Martell the second-largest cognac producer after Hennessy, with grapes from the Borderies and Troncais oak casks.

So Martell cognac is the oldest big house, and people searching for Martell liquor or Martell alcohol want one of the bottles below. A bottle may carry the words Fondee en 1715 on the label, and a Martell cognac fondee en 1715 price search leads to the standard range here. Our catalogue lists ten Martell bottles, and we do not count the two ultra rare ones in the table.

What Is Martell Cordon Bleu?

Martell Cordon Bleu is the house's best-known premium cognac. Wikipedia says it was introduced in 1912 and was for many years the world's highest-selling premium cognac, and that the house's VSOP was first created in 1831. Searches for Martell Cordon Bleu cognac or Cordon Bleu Martell lead to our Martell Cordon Bleu at $149.99, and the Cordon Bleu Extra is $499.99. We could not verify the age or blend of either from the page, so we give only the catalogue prices.

Wikipedia also lists the cognac grades set by the cognac board: VS at least two years, VSOP at least four, Napoleon at least six, XO at least ten since April 2018, and XXO at least fourteen. A search for Martell XXO therefore means the oldest grade, which we do not currently list as a standard bottle. Our cognac cocktails guide shows how the younger grades are used.

Which Martell Should You Buy?

Decide by use. For mixing in a highball or a simple cocktail, the VS at $34.99 is enough, and it puts about $2.33 of cognac in a Sidecar made to the IBA's 50 ml measure. For stirred drinks and light sipping, the VSOP at $59.99 adds age for a modest step. For sipping, the Cordon Bleu at $149.99 is the house's classic premium bottle and the XO at $199.99 is the older grade, at least ten years under the cognac board's rules.

The special bottles are bought for the story and the box. The Cohiba Edition at $349.99 and L'Or de Jean Martell at $2,499.99 are priced for rarity and presentation, and we have no verified detail beyond the catalogue entries, so ask us to confirm the edition before ordering. A sensible path is to buy the VSOP first, taste it carefully, and decide from your own notes whether the Cordon Bleu or XO is worth the step.

Because Martell is the second-largest producer, bottles are widely available, so compare prices across sellers and check that you are comparing the same size and the same grade. A 700 ml bottle priced in euros or pounds is not the same value as a 750 ml bottle priced in dollars, so always work out the price per pour before you decide that one listing is cheaper than another, and remember that gift boxes and limited editions add to the price without adding age.

The Best Way to Drink Martell Cognac

Martell's own channel has a short film, Ask Martell - HOW TO DRINK COGNAC PROPERLY, and the basics are simple. Use a tulip glass or a small snifter, pour about an ounce, and let it rest for a minute. Nose it gently, take a small first sip, and add a drop of water if you like. The best way to drink Martell cognac is the way you enjoy it, and a VS also works in a highball.

A good test is a three-glass flight of the VS, the VSOP and the XO, which together cost $294.97 as bottles. Pour half an ounce each and taste from the youngest to the oldest. Many drinkers find the VS bright, the VSOP rounder and the XO deeper, as the grades suggest. We have not poured these bottles ourselves for this guide, so we do not quote tasting notes. Keep a glass of water nearby and keep your notes with the bottles.

Whichever you choose, keep the bottle upright and sealed away from heat and light, keep the receipt and order number in case a return is needed within the return window, and make sure a gift recipient is 21 or older and enjoys cognac.

Mistakes to Avoid

A few errors come up often with bottles like these:

  • Using an XO in a Sidecar. The depth of a ten year old cognac is lost under lemon and liqueur.
  • Assuming the dates are settled. Wikipedia gives conflicting years for the Seagram and Pernod Ricard changes.
  • Confusing Martell the cognac with other uses of the name. Check the product page.
  • Paying for packaging. Limited editions such as the Cohiba Edition cost more for the presentation.
  • Skipping the grade. VSOP is at least four years, XO at least ten.

One more point is storage. Keep bottles upright, away from heat and light, and reseal them after each pour. If you are buying as a gift, make sure the recipient is 21 or older and enjoys the style, and keep the receipt and order number in case a return is needed within the return window.
