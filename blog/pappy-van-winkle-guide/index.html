What the Distillery Says About Each Age

Buffalo Trace Distillery publishes the proof, mash and tasting notes for each Pappy bottling, and these are the facts worth knowing before you spend four figures.

  • Pappy Van Winkle 15 Year. 107 proof (53.5%). Buffalo Trace describes it as crafted from the exclusive Van Winkle family wheated recipe and matured undisturbed for 15 years in deep-charred heavy oak. Aroma of caramel corn and vanilla, flavour of leather, oak and complex fruitiness, and a finish of spice and oak tannins. See the official 15 Year page.
  • Pappy Van Winkle 20 Year. 90.4 proof (45.2%), a wheated mash. The distillery lists an intense taste, smooth with a citrus zest note, with some sherry notes, dried fruits and vanilla, and a long, elegant finish.
  • Pappy Van Winkle 23 Year. 95.6 proof (47.8%), a wheated mash. Notes of caramel, ripe apples, cherries, oak and tobacco with a hint of chocolate, and a long-lasting sweet caramel finish, per the official 23 Year page.

Two things stand out. The 15 is bottled at a much higher strength than the 20 and 23, which is why it can taste bigger for less money. And all three use a wheated mash, which means wheat replaces rye as the secondary grain. That is the answer to a common search, pappy van winkle mash bill: Buffalo Trace lists it as a wheated mash and does not publish grain percentages.

The Van Winkle Story in Brief

The Buffalo Trace Distillery brand page explains where the name comes from. It says the Van Winkle family’s involvement in bourbon began in the late 1800s, that in 1935 Pappy opened the Stitzel-Weller Distillery in Louisville, Kentucky, and that his son Julian Jr. continued the legacy until 1972, when the distillery was sold. It also says the family entered a joint venture with Buffalo Trace Distillery in 2002, and describes the family’s focus as producing high-quality wheated bourbon. You can read the account on the Buffalo Trace Van Winkle page.

The brand also lists a family of bourbons beyond the three Pappy bottles: the Old Rip Van Winkle 10 Year, the Van Winkle Special Reserve 12 Year, a 13 year rye, and a 25 year old bourbon mentioned among its unique facts. Julian Van Winkle explains the family history himself in a conversation on the Pappy & Company channel, Pappy Van Winkle 23-Year Bourbon and Family History with Julian Van Winkle, from Pappy & Company. It is a brand-produced video, so treat it as the family’s own account.

Pappy Van Winkle 23 Price and the Older Bottles

The pappy van winkle 23 price is $4,499.99 for the Family Reserve at 47.8%. That is a little under $180 per ounce, and it is the oldest of the three Pappy bottlings listed on the Buffalo Trace brand page. Our catalog also lists older and rarer Pappy items at much higher prices, including a 23 Year Collector Edition at $8,999.99 and a vintage 1990s bottling at $185,000. These are listed as individual items, so read each listing and confirm the details before you buy.

The pappy van winkle 20 year price is $2,999.99, which makes the 20 the middle rung. At 45.2% it is bottled at a lower strength than the 15, so you pay for age and the family name rather than for a bigger pour. If you want the best balance of price, strength and age, the 15 Year is generally the sweet spot, while the 23 is the one for collectors and for a once-only occasion.

One rule of thumb helps: buy the bottle you will actually open. A $4,500 bottle that never leaves its box has a different purpose than one you share, and the right choice depends on which you want. Our companion piece, the Pappy Van Winkle 23 Year guide, looks at that bottle on its own.

The 10 Year: Old Rip Van Winkle Price

People often search for the pappy van winkle 10 year price, but the 10 Year in the family is sold as Old Rip Van Winkle, and Buffalo Trace lists it on the same brand page as the Pappy bottles. The old rip van winkle 10 year price in our catalog is $995, which works out to about $40 an ounce. That makes it the cheapest way into the Van Winkle family, and a good way to see the house style before you move up to a 15.

The brand page also lists a Special Reserve 12 Year, which we do not currently stock. If you are comparing the lower rungs, remember that a younger bottle is not a lesser bottle, only a different point on the age ladder. The Old Rip 10 Year is the answer for someone who wants the family’s wheated recipe without paying for 15 years or more.

Why Pappy Costs So Much

People also search pappy van winkle why so expensive, and the honest answer has several parts that you can check against the facts above:

  • Age. The oldest regular bottling is 23 years old, and bourbon aged that long is rare, as the distillery notes in describing its depth of flavour.
  • Limited supply. Buffalo Trace calls the 23 Year an exceptionally rare, limited-edition bourbon.
  • Demand. Strong demand for a very small number of bottles pushes the price of every bottle above what the whisky alone would cost.
  • Brand history. The Stitzel-Weller heritage and the family name add value that the liquid cannot.

Because supply is tight, there is no easy trick for how to find pappy van winkle at a low price. Buffalo Trace publishes a Product Availability page for its brands and its gift shop sells some of its spirits with purchasing limits that depend on availability, so check the distillery’s own pages if you want to ask whether you can buy pappy van winkle at the distillery. Whatever the route, a price far below the ranges in this guide is a warning, which our guide to buying authentic whisky online explains in detail.
