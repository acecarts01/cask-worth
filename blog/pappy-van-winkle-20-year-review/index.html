Pappy Van Winkle 20 Year Review: Official Notes, Proof, Price and How It Compares

The Van Winkle Story

Buffalo Trace’s Van Winkle page tells the history. It says the family’s bourbon story began in the late 1800s with Julian P. “Pappy” Van Winkle Sr., a traveling salesman for W.L. Weller and Sons. In 1935, at age 61, he opened the Stitzel-Weller Distillery in Louisville, Kentucky, and he stayed active in the industry until his death in 1965 at age 91.

His son Julian Jr. continued the family legacy until 1972, when the distillery was sold. In 1981, Julian III revived the family brand, focusing on high-quality wheated bourbon, and the Van Winkles entered a joint venture with Buffalo Trace Distillery in 2002. The page says Julian III added the 12, 15, 20 and 23 year labels and a 13 year rye to the lineup. If you search for a Stitzel-Weller bourbon buy, note that the present Van Winkle bourbon is produced under the Buffalo Trace joint venture, so it is not the same as old Stitzel-Weller stock.

Wheated means wheat replaces rye as the secondary grain after corn. Our Pappy Van Winkle guide covers the full range, and our Pappy Van Winkle 23 Year review looks at the oldest regular bottle.

Pappy Van Winkle 20 Year Review: Official Notes

On its Pappy Van Winkle 20 Year page, Buffalo Trace calls it a rich whiskey with an exceptionally smooth profile. The palate is described as intense and tantalizing, with a citrus zest note, sherry, dried fruit and vanilla, and the finish as long and elegant. The page does not describe the nose separately, so we do not either.

The page lists 24 awards, including a 2019 gold from the San Francisco World Spirits Competition and a 2018 five-star rating from F. Paul Pacult’s Spirit Journal. It also quotes Pacult’s score of 99. Scores are one opinion, and each year’s release uses different barrels, so a review is a guide, not a guarantee. We have not poured the current bottle ourselves, so these are the producer’s notes. For a spoken review, Oil and Whiskey’s Pappy Van Winkle 20 Year Whiskey Review is a useful companion.

Proof: 20 Year vs 15 Year

Buffalo Trace lists the 20 Year at 90.4 proof, or 45.2% ABV. That matches our catalogue. It is easy to confuse with the 15 Year, which Buffalo Trace lists at 107 proof, or 53.5% ABV, with a nose of caramel corn and vanilla, a palate of leather, oak and complex fruitiness, and a smooth finish with spice and oak tannins. Some older write-ups mix the two figures, so check which bottle a source is describing.

In practice, the 20 Year is the gentler of the two in strength, so it needs less water, while the 15 Year is more concentrated. A drinker who wants more heat and body may prefer the 15, and one who wants the older, softer style may prefer the 20.

Whichever you choose, check the label for the strength and the release year before you buy, since annual batches can differ.

The 20 Year Compared With 15 and 23

The Pappy Van Winkle 20 Year Family Reserve at $2,999.99 sits between two neighbours. The Pappy Van Winkle 15 Year at $1,199.99 and 53.5% is the lower-priced step, and the Pappy Van Winkle 23 Year Family Reserve at $4,499.99 and 47.8% is the older one. Anyone planning a Van Winkle Family Reserve buy should note that the 20 and 23 Year carry the Family Reserve name in our catalogue. Beneath all three, the Old Rip Van Winkle 10 Year at $995 and 45% is the entry to the Van Winkle family in our catalogue.

A sensible approach for most buyers is to choose by what you want to taste, not by the label alone. The 15 Year is stronger and more concentrated, the 20 Year is softer with sherry and dried fruit in Buffalo Trace’s notes, and the 23 Year is older still. If you want a wheated bourbon at a far lower price, a wheated barrel proof bottle such as William Larue Weller is another route.

How to Taste a 20 Year Old Bourbon

A bottle at this price deserves a plan. Pour about half an ounce into a tulip glass and let it rest for a few minutes before you nose it. Buffalo Trace gives a checklist for the palate: citrus zest, sherry, dried fruit and vanilla. Use that list to look for each in turn, and write down what you find before reading anyone else’s notes.

At 45.2%, the 20 Year is not a cask strength bottle, so you may not need water, but a single drop is worth trying once to see whether it opens the aroma. Take a small first sip and hold it on the tongue. The finish is described as long and elegant, so wait for it before judging. Serve it at room temperature, away from strong food, and keep a glass of water nearby.

If you own a lower-proof comparison, such as a younger wheated bourbon, pour it beside the 20 Year and taste one after the other. The difference in softness and depth is easier to understand that way than from any review. Keep a short note of what you tasted, because it is the best record of whether the bottle lived up to its price for you. Revisit the note a month later and compare it with your first impression.

Mistakes to Avoid With a Bottle This Rare

Collectors and first-time buyers repeat the same few errors with bottles at this level:

  • Confusing the 15 and 20 Year strengths. The 15 Year is 107 proof and the 20 Year is 90.4 proof, so a source that mixes them is describing the wrong bottle.
  • Trusting a review of a different year. Each release uses different barrels, so check which year a review covers.
  • Skipping provenance. Rare bottles attract counterfeits, so ask where the bottle came from.
  • Storing it badly. Keep the bottle upright, away from heat and light, and reseal it after each pour.
  • Buying only for the label. If you want to know whether you like wheated bourbon, try a lower-priced bottle first.

One more point is expectation. A bottle that costs $2,999.99 is a purchase for enjoyment, display or collecting, not a promise of future prices, and we do not suggest it as an investment. Buy it because you want to drink it or own it, share a pour with someone who will appreciate it, and keep the box and any paperwork. If you are gifting it, make sure the recipient is 21 or older and enjoys bourbon, since a bottle like this is not a casual gift.
