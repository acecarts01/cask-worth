Gift Rules to Check Before You Order

Two rules shape most business gifts in the United States, and both are worth a minute before you spend. First, the IRS says in Publication 463 that you can deduct no more than $25 for business gifts given directly or indirectly to each person during your tax year, and that incidental costs such as packaging, insuring and mailing are generally not counted toward that limit. So a $59.99 gift set is a relationship decision, not mainly a tax decision, and you should ask your accountant how it applies to you.

Second, some recipients cannot accept much at all. Under 5 CFR 2635.204, federal employees may accept unsolicited gifts worth $20 or less from a single source on a single occasion, up to $50 in a calendar year, and the exception does not cover cash. Many private companies set their own limits too. If you are not sure, ask the recipient’s office or choose a different kind of thank-you.

Alcohol adds one more point: the recipient must be 21 or older, and Caskworth applies a 21+ age check on every order. This is general information, not legal or tax advice.

Client Gifts: Woodford and Macallan

It pairs Woodford Reserve Distiller’s Select, listed at 45.2% ABV, with two signature glasses, so the recipient can pour a drink straight away and nothing else needs to be bought. Our catalogue notes dried fruit, vanilla and spice. It is also a gift with a built-in invitation to share a drink with a colleague.

When the relationship deserves more, the Macallan Discovery Set at $149.99 includes three Macallan expressions, the 12 Double Cask, 12 Sherry Oak and 15 Double Cask, with premium glassware. It reads as generous without being a single expensive bottle, and it lets the recipient compare three styles from one distillery. Check the product page for the exact bottle sizes before you buy.

VIP and Retirement Gifts

For a VIP client or a retiring executive, a set with more bottles and glassware signals care. The Scotch Explorer Set at $399.99 holds Glenfiddich 12, Macallan 12 Double Cask and Laphroaig 10 with crystal glasses, covering three styles of Scotch in one box. It suits someone who likes whisky but whose exact taste you do not know well enough to bet on a single bottle.

A retirement whiskey gift often works best when it is smooth and easy to share. The Irish Whiskey Trio at $349.99 contains Jameson Black Barrel, Redbreast 12 and Green Spot, described in our catalogue as three of Ireland’s finest with tasting notes. If you want to buy an Irish whiskey trio as a gift, it is the set to start with, and its gentler style suits someone stepping back from day-to-day work.

The Bourbon Foundations Set and the American Rye Masters Set, both $349.99 and $399.99 respectively, are better when you know the recipient prefers American whiskey. If you do not know, choose Scotch or Irish, which have the widest appeal.

Presentation matters at this level. Ask for the gift to arrive in its original box, add a card that names a shared memory or project, and avoid including a price tag. A short message such as “thank you for a great year” is enough.

If you give gifts every year, a short written policy saves time and avoids awkward comparisons between recipients. Here is a simple structure you can adapt, using the sets above:

Write the tiers down once, share them with whoever places the orders, and review them each year. A clear ladder helps when you need to explain why one client received a different gift from another, and it keeps spending predictable. It also gives you a ready answer when an assistant asks what to order for a new contact.

Keep notes on what each recipient received last year, so that you do not repeat the same set. A client who received the Woodford set one year might receive the Macallan Discovery Set the next, which makes the relationship feel as if it is growing. For people who do not drink alcohol, set a different gift aside before you place the order, and never assume that a whiskey gift will be welcome by default. Finally, check each recipient’s own gift policy, since some firms ask staff to decline gifts above a small value.

Common Gifting Mistakes

Most corporate gifting problems are avoidable. Check this list before you order:

  • Ignoring the recipient’s rules. Some employers cap gifts, as the federal $20 and $50 limits show.
  • Assuming everyone drinks. If you are unsure, choose a different gift for that person.
  • Skipping the age check. Alcohol deliveries need a 21+ recipient.
  • Ordering too late. Order before the daily cutoff for same-day delivery where available.
  • Choosing a style you do not know they like. A variety set is safer than a single bottle.
  • Forgetting the card. A personal note matters more than the price.

One more point is timing. Peak gifting periods fill delivery schedules, so place orders early, and confirm the delivery address is staffed, because an unattended business address can delay a delivery that needs an adult recipient.
