Buy Weller Full Proof: What the Bottle Is

Buffalo Trace Distillery’s W.L. Weller Full Proof page lists it at 114 proof (57%), made from a wheated mash, and describes it as bottled at 114 proof, the same strength it entered the barrel. The page does not state an age. The brand page for W.L. Weller says the brand is built on the pioneering idea of swapping rye for wheat to produce a softer, smoother bourbon, and that William Larue Weller, born in 1825, founded W.L. Weller & Brothers in 1849.

Wheated simply means wheat replaces rye as the secondary grain, which tends to give a softer, rounder whiskey. The federal rules for bourbon still apply: at least 51% corn, charred new oak and nothing added but water, as set out in 27 CFR 5.143.

What Full Proof Means

Full proof is not the same as barrel proof. VinePair’s explainer says full proof usually means bottled at the same proof the spirit had before it entered a barrel, as Buffalo Trace uses the term, and that the TTB does not regulate it. Barrel proof, by contrast, means bottled at the strength the whiskey had when it came out of the barrel. Buffalo Trace’s own wording for Weller Full Proof is the entry-strength definition, so this is a barrel entry proof bourbon.

That is why people search for a barrel entry proof bourbon buy: the idea is to taste a whiskey at the strength it started at. Our cask strength vs barrel proof guide explains the terms in detail. For a quick independent review, Bourbon Pursuit’s Weller Full Proof Review in 60 Seconds gives a short view.

Tasting Notes From Buffalo Trace

The distillery gives separate notes: a nose of vanilla, dark cherries and caramel; a palate with a balance of caramel and toasted oak; and a finish said to be long, with notes of crème brûlée and chocolate. The page also lists 49 awards, including a Double Gold at the Global Whiskey Challenge in 2024 and a Double Gold at the North American Bourbon and Whiskey Competition in 2024.

In practice, expect a sweet, dessert-like profile with oak at 57% ABV, so add a few drops of water if it feels hot. Treat awards as one signal rather than a guarantee that you will like it. A dessert-like style such as this suits drinkers who enjoy sweeter bourbons and the softness that wheat brings, and it may suit rye-bourbon fans less, so it helps to know your own taste before spending $219.99. If you usually prefer spicy bourbons, try the Antique 107 first, which costs far less and shows the wheated style at a similar strength.

How to Serve 114 Proof Bourbon

At 57% ABV, Weller Full Proof is a strong whiskey, and it rewards a careful approach. Pour a small measure of about an ounce into a tulip or Glencairn glass and let it sit for a couple of minutes. Nose it gently, because the alcohol can sting if you push your face into the glass. Take a first tiny sip neat to meet the whiskey as it is, then add water a few drops at a time and taste after each addition.

Many drinkers find that a little water softens the heat and brings out the sweetness the distillery describes. You can also use one large ice cube, which dilutes slowly. Avoid mixing it into a busy cocktail, because at this price the character of the whiskey is what you are paying for. Store the bottle upright, away from heat and sunlight.

A Two-Bottle Weller Tasting

If you want to understand what the extra strength adds, taste the Antique 107 and the Full Proof together. They cost $84.99 and $219.99, a total of $304.98. Both are wheated, and Buffalo Trace lists them at 107 proof (53.5%) and 114 proof (57%), so the difference is seven proof points and the entry-proof approach.

Pour half an ounce of each, taste neat and then with a few drops of water, and note the sweetness, the oak and the finish. Buffalo Trace describes the Antique 107 as having sweet and floral notes with vanilla and sharp spicy tones, so you can see whether the Full Proof’s caramel and toasted oak suit you better. If you cannot tell them apart, the Antique 107 is the better value, and if you can, you have learned what you are paying for.

Common Mistakes

A few mistakes cost buyers money:

  • Paying a large markup without checking. Compare with a retailer’s list price before you pay more on the secondary market.
  • Assuming full proof means the same as barrel proof. Buffalo Trace uses full proof for entry strength, which is a different idea.
  • Ignoring the strength on a catalogue listing. Our own catalogue shows 60% for the Weller 12 where the distillery says 45%, so always check the label.
  • Buying to resell. Whisky prices can fall as well as rise, and this is general information, not financial advice.

The simplest safeguard is to buy from a seller who explains the bottle and takes responsibility for problems, and to keep the invoice and photographs of the label and fill level. Finally, enjoy the bottle: a whiskey like this is made to be tasted, shared and talked about, and the best way to get value from a $219.99 purchase is to open it with friends and compare notes, so that the experience is part of what you paid for, and so that you can compare your impressions with a second taster over a relaxed evening.
