Ultra Rare Whisky for Sale and the Auction Records That Set the Bar

Ultra Rare Whisky for Sale: The Verified Auction Records

Many lists of record whisky sales mix auction results with private deals and claims, so we stick to results that a primary source confirms. Guinness World Records lists the most expensive whisky sold at auction as the Macallan Valerio Adami 60 Year Old, a 1926 Macallan, at £2,187,500 including premium, sold to an anonymous bidder at Sotheby’s in London on 18 November 2023. It says Macallan produced 40 bottles of this whisky and that 12 were designed by Valerio Adami. You can read the entry on the Guinness World Records page.

Each of these is a rare whisky world record of its kind, and each is a record breaking whisky sale in the sense that it beat what came before. Note that the figures include the buyer’s premium, which is why a single sale can appear at different amounts in different articles. Auction Terms Explained

Auction results are easier to read when you know the words. These are the terms that matter:

  • Hammer price. The winning bid, before any fees. In the 2023 Macallan sale, Whisky Advocate reported a winning bid of £1.75 million.
  • Buyer’s premium. A fee added to the hammer price. £1.75 million plus 25% is £2,187,500, which is the total Guinness records.
  • Lot. One item or group of items offered in the sale. Whisky Hammer’s April 2022 sale included more than 2,000 lots.
  • Estimate. The auction house’s expected price range. Sotheby’s article says the 2023 bottle sold for nearly twice its high estimate of £1.2 million.
  • Provenance. The documented history of a bottle: where it has been and who has owned it.

Two results with the same headline number can mean different things depending on which of these terms the number refers to, so always ask which one a figure describes before comparing it with anything else, including a retail listing.

How the Bottle Record Climbed

Looking at the record as a timeline shows how quickly the market moved at the top. The sequence from Sotheby’s and Guinness is: £5,000 in 1987, £1,200,000 in 2018, £1,452,000 in October 2019 and £2,187,500 in November 2023. The gap between 2019 and 2023 is about 50% over four years, and the gap between 1987 and 2018 is roughly 240 times over 31 years.

Those are results for specific bottles from the same famous release, not an index of whisky prices. An index tracks many bottles over time, and the Knight Frank figures show it can move down as well as up. A record also reflects who happened to be in the room: one bidder can set a result that no one else in the market would match.

If you follow whisky news, treat each new record as one data point. It tells you that at least one buyer was willing to pay that amount for a rare, well-documented bottle, and it tells you very little about what an ordinary bottle is worth.

The Bottle Record: Macallan Adami 1926

Sotheby’s history of the 1926 Macallan says it was matured in a single sherry cask for six decades, with 40 bottles bottled in 1986: 12 with Peter Blake labels, 12 with Valerio Adami labels, up to 14 with the Fine & Rare label and 2 with none. Its article Revisiting the Most Expensive Whisky Ever Sold records an earlier sale at £5,000 in New York in 1987, which the article says earned a Guinness record as the world’s most expensive spirit, and then a climb through £1,452,000 in 2019 to the 2023 result. That is a rise of roughly 440 times in 36 years for one small group of bottles, and it is the kind of figure that drives headlines.

Sotheby’s has also released a short film, The World’s Most Valuable Whisky: The Macallan Adami 1926, if you want to see the bottle.

The Cask Record

Records are not limited to bottles. Scottish Financial News reported that a 33 year old single malt from The Macallan, filled on 5 May 1988 in a 374-litre refill butt, sold through Whisky Hammer for US$1,295,500 including buyer’s premium, when the auction closed on 24 April 2022. The report calls it the most expensive cask of whisky ever sold at auction, and it was part of an online sale that included more than 2,000 lots. You can read it in the Scottish Financial News report.

A cask sale is a different kind of asset from a bottle. A buyer of a cask owns a barrel of maturing whisky that still has to be bottled, stored and insured. Casks carry extra risks for ordinary buyers, including storage, insurance, bottling costs and the fraud problem covered in our guide to cask investment scams.

What the Records Tell a Buyer

Records are a snapshot of what one buyer paid on one day. Read them carefully:

  • They are exceptions. The bottles that set records are rare ones with a famous name, a small production and a documented history, such as 40 bottles from one cask.
  • Premiums matter. A result that includes the buyer’s premium is not what the seller received.
  • Prices can fall as well as rise. Knight Frank data reported by Outlook Business showed whisky bottle prices down 10.9% in late 2025. See our whisky vs stock market investment guide.
  • A record is not a forecast. It describes what happened, not what will happen.

If you are looking for a macallan auction price to compare with a listing, check the release, the label, the date and whether the figure includes premium, because small differences change the number a lot. A fill level a little lower, a label with a mark or a missing box can all move a price, and the same release can sell for noticeably different amounts in different sales, so one result is never a fixed price for the whole release. Use several results, from several dates, and treat the spread between them as the real picture. If a seller quotes a single record as proof that a bottle is worth a particular sum, ask for the source and the date.

How to Read an Ultra Rare Listing

Before you buy any ultra rare whisky, use this checklist:

  1. Confirm the exact release against the producer or auction house information: name, age, year, strength, bottle number and packaging.
  2. Ask for provenance and any paperwork, and keep it.
