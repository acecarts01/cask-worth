Yamazaki 55 Year: Japan's Rarest Whisky and Its $600,000 Legacy

By Caskworth Editorial July 11, 20268 min readUltra Rare Japanese

In 2021, Suntory released 100 bottles of Yamazaki 55 Year — Japan's oldest and most aged single malt. Each bottle carries a price tag of $600,000 and represents over half a century of uninterrupted maturation in the mountains south of Kyoto.

Japan's Oldest Single Malt

The Yamazaki 55 Year was distilled in the 1960s and allowed to mature undisturbed for over five decades in a combination of American white oak, Spanish sherry and Japanese Mizunara oak casks. Only 100 bottles were ever produced for the global market.

Tasting Notes

Those privileged to taste the 55 Year describe an overwhelming complexity — layers of sandalwood incense from Mizunara oak, Kyoto cherry blossom, ancient dried rose petals, rich dried tropical fruits and a palate that evolves continuously for up to two hours. Nothing else in the whisky world compares.

Investment Value

Every bottle sold immediately on release. The secondary market now values authenticated examples above $600,000 and prices continue to rise as Japan's whisky output remains constrained by strict quality standards.

The Accessible Yamazaki Range

The Yamazaki 25 Year ($4,499.99) and Yamazaki 35 Year ($14,999.99) are available at Caskworth — the closest you can get to the 55 Year heritage with same-day delivery to 33 U.S. states.
