What Suntory Says About Yamazaki 55 Year

The House of Suntory’s own announcement, carried on Kyodo News PR Wire, introduces Yamazaki 55 Years Old as a highly limited edition for global travel retail, describing this year’s 100-bottle collection. It says the blend combines whisky distilled in 1960 under Shinjiro Torii and aged in Mizunara casks with whisky distilled in 1964 under Keizo Saji and aged in white oak casks. The release lists a recommended retail price of $60,000 and names select global travel retail locations including London, Paris, Hong Kong, Taipei, Dubai and Singapore.

Yamazaki itself is Japan’s first malt whisky distillery, founded in 1923 by Shinjiro Torii, according to the House of Suntory page on Yamazaki 12 Years Old. That history is why a whisky distilled in 1960 under Torii carries the weight it does. Suntory’s film coverage of the launch, such as Yamazaki 55 Year Old Launch from The Whiskey Wash, shows the bottle.

Note what the release does not say. The announcement does not list the bottling strength, so confirm it on the label of any bottle you consider. Our catalogue lists 41.6%.

Yamazaki 55 Year Price: Launch vs Listing

Anyone looking at a yamazaki 55 for sale listing, or wanting to buy the rarest japanese whisky in the market, should compare figures carefully. People searching for the yamazaki 55 year price will see very different figures, and they are not contradictory once you know what each one is. Suntory’s recommended retail price for the 2021 travel retail release was $60,000. Caskworth’s catalogue lists the Yamazaki 55 Year 2021 Release at $600,000, which is ten times the launch figure. A launch price is what the producer asks through its own channel, while a listing for a bottle that is already out in the market reflects what collectors will pay when supply is fixed at about 100 bottles.

That gap is the reason to ask questions before any purchase:

  • Is this a bottle from the 2021 release, and how can that be shown?
  • What is its history? Where has it been since it left the producer’s channel?
  • What paperwork exists, such as the original box, serial or bottle number and any certificate?
  • What is the exact price and availability today? Prices can change, and for a listing at this level you should contact Caskworth before ordering.

Tasting Notes From Suntory

The announcement gives a short description: a deep amber colour, a robust aroma redolent of sandalwood and well-ripened fruit, a sweet, slightly bitter and woody palate, and a finish that is slightly bitter yet sweet and rich. Those are the producer’s own words, and they are the ones to rely on. Any more elaborate description you see online, including flowers, spices or specific fruits, comes from individual tasters and not from Suntory.

What can be said from the description is that the style is dark and wood-led, as you would expect from whisky of this age, with sandalwood and ripe fruit at the front. Few people will ever taste it, so if you are curious about the house style, the younger Yamazaki bottles below are the practical way in. Reviews of the 55 are rare for the same reason, since very few bottles have been opened, so be cautious of any detailed review that does not say where the bottle came from.

The Yamazaki Timeline

The dates in Suntory’s announcement tell a story of three eras. Yamazaki was founded in 1923 by Shinjiro Torii, which makes it Japan’s first malt whisky distillery. The 1960 whisky in the blend was distilled under Torii, so it comes from the founder’s own era. The 1964 whisky was distilled under Keizo Saji, who succeeded him, and was aged in white oak, while the 1960 whisky was aged in Mizunara casks. Blending the two eras and the two kinds of wood is the idea behind the release.

By the time of the 2021 release, the youngest of those whiskies had been in cask for more than five decades. The whisky in the bottle does not change after bottling, so a 55 year old stays a 55 year old however long the bottle waits, a point made by the Scotch Whisky Association for Scotch and true of any whisky. That is why the bottle’s condition, box and documentation matter more than any further ageing.

What Counts as Japanese Whisky

When you buy any Japanese whisky, it helps to know that the word Japanese has a definition. The House of Suntory’s page on the Japanese whisky definition says that for members of the Japan Spirits & Liqueurs Makers Association, Japanese whisky must be matured for at least three years in Japan and bottled at no less than 40% alcohol by volume, and that the rules apply to members for bottles from 1 April 2024. A Yamazaki bottle from a Suntory distillery falls within that framework.

The definition is a floor, as with Scotch. It does not describe the quality of a particular bottle, and it is a producers’ association standard, not a government law. For a rare release the details that matter are the ones on the producer’s own announcement: the years, the casks and the number of bottles. Use the definition to understand labels on younger bottles, and the announcement to understand the 55.

Checks Before You Buy Yamazaki 55 Year

For a suntory yamazaki 55 buy, or any bottle at this level, work through this list:

  1. Confirm the release. Compare the listing with Suntory’s announcement: 100-bottle collection, 1960 and 1964 components, 2021.
  2. Ask for the bottle number and packaging. A numbered bottle in its original box and any paperwork should be documented in the listing.
  3. Ask for provenance. A clear history is worth more than a good story.
