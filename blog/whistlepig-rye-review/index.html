WhistlePig 18 Year Review: Official Specs, Price and How the 10 and 15 Year Compare

By Caskworth Editorial Published September 10, 2026Updated November 9, 20269 min read

This WhistlePig 18 Year review uses the brand’s own words first. WhistlePig’s page calls it Double Double Rye Aged 18 Years, with a mash bill of 91% rye and 9% malted barley, double barrel ageing in American oak and Pedro Ximenez sherry casks, and a listed price of $399.99. Our catalogue lists WhistlePig 18 Year Double Malt Rye at $449.99. The WhistlePig Range and Where It Is Made

Wikipedia’s WhistlePig article says the company dates to about 2007, when Raj Bhakta bought land in Shoreham, Vermont, and that the brand launched in 2010. The property is a 500 acre farm, about 300 acres of it growing rye, with two pot stills, and production began in 2015. It says the first whiskey was sourced: master distiller Dave Pickerell introduced an initial stock of 10 year old Canadian whiskey from the Alberta Premium distillery, and suppliers have also included MGP of Indiana.

That answers the question many buyers ask, where is WhistlePig made. Some bottles have used sourced whiskey and some come from the Vermont distillery, so the label and the brand’s page for each bottle matter. The Wikipedia article adds that the distillery is not open to the public, so anyone hoping for a WhistlePig tasting room should check the brand’s site for current visitor options.

The article lists the range: the 10 year Small Batch Rye, PiggyBack Rye at 6 years, Farmstock, the 12 year Old World Cask Finish, the 15 year Estate Oak, the 18 year Double Malt, the annual Boss Hog, a 21 year Béhôlden single malt, and bourbons from 2021 and 2022. Our Alberta Premium rye guide covers the Canadian side of that story.

WhistlePig 18 Year Review: Official Notes

On the WhistlePig 18 Year page, the brand calls it its oldest expression to date and a bold, peppery rye. The mash bill is 91% rye and 9% malted barley, which the page says adds soft, floral and earthy notes, and the whiskey is double barrel aged in American oak and PX sherry casks. The nose is described as sweet and aromatic with toasted almonds, caramel and subtle smoked maple, the palate as warm, barely sweet and grassy with a lingering soft honey note, and the finish as long with dried fruit.

The page lists a gold medal from the New York World Wine & Spirits Competition and says each bottle comes with a handmade Vermont glass topper. It does not state a proof. Our catalogue lists 46% ABV, and a WhistlePig 18 reviewed by trade sites is commonly given at 46%, but check the label on the bottle you receive.

For a spoken companion, Fred Minnick’s Blind Rye Whiskey Tasting: WhistlePig 10 Year Rye Whiskey shows how the 10 Year tastes, which helps put the 18 in context. We have not poured the 18 ourselves, so these are the producer’s notes.

Conflicts to Check Before You Buy

Sources do not agree on the 18 Year. Trade reviews report a different mash bill from the brand’s own 91% rye and 9% malted barley, and some retailer pages describe different casks. We cannot reconcile them from here, so we give the brand’s version and flag the rest. Ask a seller which release and year a bottle is, and read the label.

The price differs too. The brand lists $399.99 for the 18 Year on its page, and our catalogue lists $449.99. Retailers price above and below both, so compare like with like. A similar check applies to the Farmstock Rye Crop No.003, which our catalogue lists at 40% under a Canadian subcategory, while Wikipedia describes Farmstock as a blend of Canadian and on-site rye. If a bottle matters to you, ask us to confirm before you order.

Finally, the old claim that the 10 Year is a 100% rye mash does not appear on the brand’s 10 Year page, which calls it a high rye whiskey without a percentage. Wikipedia attributes the 100% rye claim to the PiggyBack Rye.

WhistlePig 10 Year and 15 Year

The brand’s 10 Year page calls it the Quintessential Rye at 100 proof, which is 50% ABV, with a nose of allspice, orange peel, anise, oak and caramel, a sweet palate of caramel and vanilla followed by rye spice and mint, and a long butterscotch finish. It lists $79.99, matching ours, and it is the whistle pig 10 year rye, sold as the Small Batch Rye, that most buyers start with, and a search for a whistle pig 10 year small batch rye leads to this bottle. The WhistlePig 10 Year is the lowest-priced bottle here.

The WhistlePig 15 Year Vermont Estate Oak at $249.99 and 46% is the step up, finished in oak from the brand’s own farm according to our catalogue. People who search for a whistle pig 15 are usually weighing it against the 10 and the 18, and the cost difference is large: the 15 costs about three times the 10, and the 18 is about $200 more again. We list the WhistlePig 18 Year Double Malt Rye at $449.99.

Searches for a whistle pig 12 year lead to the Old World Cask Finish, which Wikipedia says is finished in Port, Sauternes and Madeira barrels. We do not currently list it.

What Is Whistle Pig Bourbon?

WhistlePig is known for rye, but Wikipedia says it has also released bourbons. PiggyBack Bourbon, released in 2022, is described as 6 years old with a high-corn mash bill and a number 3 char, bottled at 100 proof. A Beyond Bonded bourbon followed from 2021, and the brand’s site lists a Snout to Tail bourbon aged 10 years. Anyone searching for WhistlePig bourbon is looking for those bottles, and a whistle pig 10 year bourbon search is usually a mix-up with the 10 Year Rye.

We do not currently list a WhistlePig bourbon. If you want one, check the brand’s page for the specific release and its strength, because the bourbons are a different product from the rye and are priced differently.

How to Taste a Rye

Pour about an ounce into a tulip glass and let it sit for a minute. Nose it gently, and look for the almonds, caramel and maple in the brand’s notes for the 18, or the allspice and orange peel for the 10. Take a small first sip, hold it on the tongue, and notice how the rye spice arrives before the sweetness. Add a drop or two of water, because rye often opens with a little dilution.

The best comparison is to pour the 10 and the 18 side by side in matching glasses and write one line for each. The 10 is stronger at 50%, and the 18 is softer at 46%, so you will taste the effect of age and strength together. Keep notes with the bottle.

Mistakes to Avoid With Rye

A few errors come up often when buying a premium rye:

  • Assuming every WhistlePig is distilled in Vermont. Sourcing varies by release, so read the label.
  • Trusting one review. Trade reviews disagree on the 18 Year’s recipe, so use the brand page as the baseline.
  • Skipping the 10 Year. At $79.99 it teaches you the house style for a fraction of the cost of the 18.
  • Comparing prices without checking the release. A different year or edition can cost more or less.
  • Over-diluting. A drop or two of water is plenty at 46% to 50%.

One more point is storage. Keep bottles upright, away from heat and light, and reseal them after each pour. If you are buying as a gift, a rye suits someone who likes spice, and a bottle with a clear age statement is easier to explain than a limited release. Make sure the recipient is 21 or older and enjoys whiskey, and include the receipt in case a return is needed.
