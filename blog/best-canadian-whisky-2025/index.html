Brands of Canadian Whiskey: Twelve Bottles Compared and What the Canadian Rules Say

By Caskworth Editorial Published August 30, 2026Updated November 9, 20269 min read

Brands of Canadian whiskey range from cheap everyday blends to rare aged bottles, and twelve in our catalogue run from $8.99 to $56.99. Under Canadian rules, whisky must be mashed, distilled and aged in Canada, aged in small wood vessels for at least three years, and bottled at no less than 40% ABV, and most Canadian whisky is a blend of a corn-based base whisky and flavouring whiskies, often rye. This guide compares the twelve by price and cost per pour, answers whether Crown Royal is Canadian, and maps the premium bottles above them, including Forty Creek.

What Makes It Canadian Whisky

Wikipedia's Canadian whisky article says the Food and Drugs Act regulations require the whisky to be mashed, distilled and aged in Canada, aged in small wood vessels for not less than three years, bottled at 40% alcohol by volume or more, and allowed to contain caramel and flavouring. It also says that under Canadian law, Canadian whisky and rye whisky refer to the same product, even when it contains only a small amount of rye. That is why a Canadian blended whisky is often called rye, and it is a rule that differs sharply from American rye, which must be at least 51% rye.

The article adds that most Canadian whiskies are blends, with a mostly corn-based base whisky combined with flavouring whiskies, usually rye, which can be as little as 10%. So the best Canadian rye whiskey is not necessarily a rye-heavy one, and a bottle described as 100% rye is a specific claim. Is Crown Royal Canadian?

Yes. Wikipedia's Crown Royal article describes Crown Royal as a blended Canadian whisky, owned by Diageo since 2000 and produced solely at the Gimli, Manitoba distillery on Lake Winnipeg. It says the whisky was introduced in 1939 by Samuel Bronfman for the royal tour of King George VI and Queen Elizabeth, and sold only in Canada until 1964. The original comes in a purple felt-like bag with a gold tasseled drawstring.

The same article says Crown Royal Black is 45% ABV, and that Crown Royal XO is a blend of 50 whiskies finished in cognac casks from the Limousin forest, which our catalogue lists at $469.99. People who ask whether Crown Royal is a rye whiskey are asking about the Canadian rule above: by Canadian law the terms overlap, but the article calls it a blend. We list the Crown Royal Black at $32.99 and 45%. The flavoured range is a separate set of bottles.

What Is a Canadian Whisky Brand Worth Buying?

A Canadian whisky brand is worth buying when you know what you want from it. For mixing, the lowest-priced blends such as Canadian Mist, Black Velvet and Seagram's VO do the job. For sipping, the middle of the table, from Crown Royal Black at 45% to Lot No. 40, offers more body. For a gift, a Crown Royal Deluxe is a safe, recognisable choice.

Award lists name different winners each year and are often dated, so use them as leads and check the label for the release. If you are comparing a Canadian blend with an American whiskey, remember the different rules: a Canadian rye needs no minimum rye content, which is why a side-by-side tasting is the only reliable test.

A Canadian Whisky Tasting Flight

A flight is the cheapest way to learn the range. Pour half an ounce each of Seagram's VO at $14.99, Crown Royal Deluxe at $28.99 and Crown Royal Black at $32.99, which together cost $76.97, into matching glasses, and taste them from the lowest strength to the highest. Nose each gently, take a small first sip, and add a drop of water to the 45% Black.

Write one line per glass: light or full, sweet or dry, short or long finish. Many drinkers find Canadian blends softer and lighter than bourbon, which fits the base-whisky-plus-flavouring method the rules allow. If you then add a premium bottle such as a Forty Creek, you will taste what longer ageing and careful blending add.

Keep a glass of water nearby, wait a minute between pours, and keep your notes with the bottles. Revisit the notes after a few weeks, because a second tasting often changes your ranking of the three. The point is to learn which style you prefer, so that your next purchase is a choice, not a guess.

Mistakes to Avoid

A few errors come up often with bottles like these:

  • Assuming Canadian rye means rye-heavy. The rule lets a blend with a small rye share be called rye.
  • Comparing 40% and 45% bottles as equals. Strength changes body and heat.
  • Paying premium prices without checking the release. Rare bottles vary by year and edition.
  • Using a rare bottle for mixing. A $8.99 to $14.99 blend does the job in a highball.
  • Trusting dated award lists. Check the year before you rely on one.

One more point is storage. Keep bottles upright, away from heat and light, and reseal them after each pour. If you are buying as a gift, make sure the recipient is 21 or older and enjoys the style, and keep the receipt and order number in case a return is needed within the return window.
