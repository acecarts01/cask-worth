What Is the Best Cognac for Cocktails? Four IBA Recipes and Eight Bottles With the Cost of Each Drink

What is the best cognac for cocktails? For most drinks the answer is a VS or VSOP, because the youngest brandy in a VS is at least two years old and in a VSOP at least four, which makes them lively enough to stand up to citrus and liqueurs, while an XO is better sipped. What Is the Best Cognac for Cocktails?

Wikipedia's Cognac article lists the age grades set by the cognac board: VS is the youngest brandy aged at least two years, VSOP at least four, Napoleon at least six, XO at least ten since a rule change in April 2018, and XXO at least fourteen. It also says the wine is distilled twice in copper Charentais pot stills and aged in French oak. So when a bartender says use a VS or VSOP, the reason is age and price: younger cognacs are brighter and cheaper, and a drink with lemon, liqueur or vermouth will hide the subtleties of a ten year old.

Our rule of thumb is a VSOP for stirred drinks and a VS for highballs and shaken drinks, and an XO only for sipping. The same article notes that more than 90% of cognac sold in the US market in 2008 came from four houses, Courvoisier, Hennessy, Martell and Remy Martin, which is why those names fill the table below.

The Best Cognac Cocktails: Four IBA Recipes

The IBA publishes official recipes, and four of them use cognac. Quantities are in millilitres, as the IBA lists them.

CocktailIngredientsMethodGlass
Sidecar50 ml cognac, 20 ml triple sec, 20 ml fresh lemon juiceShake with ice and strainChilled cocktail glass
Vieux Carré30 ml rye whiskey, 30 ml cognac, 30 ml sweet vermouth, 1 bar spoon Benedictine, 2 dashes Peychaud's bittersStir with ice and strainChilled cocktail glass, orange zest and cherry
Alexander30 ml cognac, 30 ml brown crème de cacao, 30 ml fresh creamShake with ice and strainChilled cocktail glass, grated nutmeg
Stinger50 ml cognac, 20 ml white crème de mentheStir with ice and strainChilled cocktail glass, optional mint leaf

The IBA Sidecar is the benchmark among cognac cocktails, and the IBA Vieux Carré is the one that pairs cognac with rye. Many recipes online differ in quantities and garnish, so treat these as the standard. The IBA's French 75 is a gin recipe, so we do not list a cognac version here. A Hennessy sidecar and other Hennessy cocktails are simply these drinks made with a Hennessy bottle, which is why a VSOP such as Hennessy VSOP Privilege is a popular choice.

For a video, The Educated Barfly's The Sidecar takes a back seat to no one shows the drink being made. People ask what the best cognac for a sidecar is, and the answer above is a VSOP.

Cognac vs Whiskey in Cocktails

Cognac vs whiskey is a common question for the Vieux Carré, which uses both, and for classics such as the Old Fashioned. Cognac is a grape spirit aged in French oak, so it tends to be softer and fruitier, while whiskey is grain-based and spicier. The Vieux Carré recipe above splits the base evenly between rye and cognac, which is a good way to see the difference in one glass.

If you already enjoy whiskey cocktails, our best whiskey for an Old Fashioned guide covers the bourbon and rye side, and trying the same drink with a cognac is an easy experiment. Our cognac vs brandy guide explains what separates cognac from other brandies.

Other Ways to Drink Cognac

You do not need a recipe to enjoy cognac. A simple highball of a VS with ginger ale or ginger beer is the easiest, and many people also pair it with iced tea. To drink cognac neat, use a tulip glass, pour about an ounce, and add a drop of water if you like. For what to mix with cognac, citrus, ginger, tea and vermouth all work. The cheaper VS bottles are fine for mixed drinks, and a VSOP or better rewards sipping.

Keep the glass at room temperature, avoid strong food while tasting, and take your time. We have not poured these bottles for this guide, so we do not quote our own tasting notes.

Tips for Better Cognac Cocktails

A few habits improve every drink on this page. Use fresh lemon juice, because bottled juice flattens a Sidecar. Measure with a jigger, because the IBA recipes depend on small ratios, and a few extra millilitres of liqueur can tip the drink out of balance. Chill the glass before you pour, and use plenty of good ice when you shake or stir, because dilution is part of the recipe.

Taste as you go. Search results for cognac drinks note that recipes and proportions vary from source to source, so treat the IBA measurements as a starting point and adjust in small amounts if your lemon is sharp or your liqueur is sweet. Keep notes on what you change, so you can repeat the drink you liked.

When you batch for a party, work out the numbers first. A 750 ml bottle gives 15 Sidecars at 50 ml each or 25 Alexanders at 30 ml each, which is more than enough for most gatherings. Mix the base in a jug in advance and shake each serving to order. Stirred drinks such as the Vieux Carré batch well with a little water added for dilution.

Mistakes to Avoid

A few errors come up often with bottles like these:

  • Using an XO in a Sidecar. The depth of a ten year old cognac is lost under lemon and liqueur.
  • Skipping the measuring. The recipes depend on ratios, so use a jigger.
  • Using bottled juice. Fresh lemon is the difference between a bright drink and a flat one.
  • Serving warm. Chill the glass and shake or stir until the tin is cold.
  • Assuming one recipe is the only one. Sources differ, so check the IBA version and adjust.

One more point is storage. Keep bottles upright, away from heat and light, and reseal them after each pour. If you are buying as a gift, make sure the recipient is 21 or older and enjoys the style, and keep the receipt and order number in case a return is needed within the return window.
