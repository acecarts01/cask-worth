Blended Scotch Explained: Johnnie Walker, Chivas Regal & Dewar's Compared

By Caskworth Editorial August 27, 20267 min readScotch Guide

Blended Scotch outsells single malt worldwide by a wide margin — here's how the three biggest names stack up tier by tier, and what you actually get as you spend more.

What Blended Scotch Actually Is

A blended Scotch combines single malt whisky from multiple distilleries with grain whisky, all mixed by a master blender to hit a consistent house flavor year after year. That consistency, plus generally lower prices than single malt, is exactly why blends dominate global Scotch sales — the goal isn't showcasing one distillery's character, it's crafting a specific, repeatable taste.

Johnnie Walker Tier by Tier

Johnnie Walker Black Label 12 Year — $34.99

Dark fruit, vanilla and smoke in the label most drinkers first encounter — a genuine benchmark for what well-made blended Scotch can deliver at an accessible price.

Johnnie Walker Double Black — $39.99

A smokier, more intense variation on Black Label built with whiskies aged in deeply charred casks — smoke, rich fruit and dark spice throughout.

Johnnie Walker Blue Label — $199.99

Honey, dried fruit and lingering smoke in Johnnie Walker's flagship, blended from rare whiskies selected for exceptional smoothness rather than a specific age statement.

Chivas Regal

Chivas Regal 12 Year — $29.99

Honey, vanilla and apple in a soft, fruit-forward blend built for easy, approachable sipping.

Chivas Regal 18 Year Gold Signature — $74.99

Dark chocolate, dried fruit and hazelnuts replace the lighter fruit of the 12 Year with real richness and length.

Dewar's

Dewar's 12 Year — $29.99

Honeyed malt, dried fruit and spice from a brand built around double-aging its blends after marrying, for extra smoothness.

Dewar's 18 Year — $69.99

Dark fruit, toffee and rich grain in one of the most consistently well-reviewed blended Scotches in this price range.
