Alberta Premium Cask Strength Rye: What the 100% Rye Is, Its Real Strength and What to Check

By Caskworth Editorial Published September 6, 2026Updated November 9, 20269 min read

Alberta Premium Cask Strength Rye is a Canadian whisky that press coverage describes as 100% rye, aged a minimum of five years and bottled at cask strength, with strengths reported between 63.5% and 66.1% depending on the release. Our catalogue lists it at $499.99 and 40% ABV, which does not match those reports, so we flag the conflict below. This guide covers what is documented about the distillery and the rye, how the strength varies, what the older 25 and 30 Year bottles are, and what to check before you buy.

What Alberta Premium Cask Strength Rye Is

The Whiskey Wash’s report on the US launch, Alberta Distillers Recently Released A Premium Cask Strength Rye Into The US, says the whisky is made from 100% Canadian prairie grains and Rocky Mountain spring water, distilled in Calgary, aged a minimum of five years and bottled at 65.1% to 66.1% in that edition. It calls it a true 100% rye whisky and gave a suggested price of about $70 for a 750 ml bottle, with the article dated 27 December 2020. A Canadian review at The Gate adds a later release at 63.5%, with a CAD $89.45 price and a double gold at the 2022 SIP Awards.

That matters because rye in Canada is a different thing from rye in the United States. American rye must be at least 51% rye under 27 CFR 5.143, while Alberta Premium is described as 100% rye, so an Alberta Premium cask strength rye whiskey is made from rye alone, with no corn or barley listed. The American rules are the baseline for comparison, and a 100% rye mash goes well beyond them.

The same Gate review says the brand has released it in limited quantity, and it describes the distiller as delighted to bring it back, which suggests a recurring release without confirming that it is annual. We therefore describe it as a limited edition and recommend you check the year on any bottle.

Alberta Cask Strength Rye Review: Tasting Notes

Two published descriptions give a picture. The Whiskey Wash describes the nose as sweet, woody and oaky with a hint of vanilla, the palate as caramel, vanilla, a touch of chocolate, rye and black currant, and the finish as spice, warm vanilla and dark fruit. The Gate describes the whisky neat as rich and almost meaty, with a lot of heat like cinnamon, then a hint of chocolate, with creamy caramel on the second sip, and on the rocks as surprisingly sweet, like dried fruit and vanilla.

Those are two reviewers’ notes from different releases, and we have not poured the bottle ourselves, so treat them as a guide. For a spoken review, On The Barrel Whiskey’s Oh Canada Cream Pie! Alberta Premium Cask Strength Canadian Rye is a useful companion, and an Alberta cask strength rye review should always say which year the bottle is.

Strength, Price and the Catalogue Conflict

Our catalogue lists Alberta Premium Cask Strength Rye at 40% ABV and $499.99. Neither figure matches the published reports. A cask strength whisky is by definition bottled far above 40%, and the reports give 63.5% to 66.1%. The 2020 launch price was about $70 and the 2022-era Canadian price CAD $89.45, so $499.99 is many times higher. We cannot tell from here whether the catalogue entry is wrong or reflects a different release, so please compare our listing with other sellers and ask us to confirm the strength and year before you order.

The strength changes by release. Retailer listings for later editions show figures such as 63.7%, 64.2% and 66%, which fits the reported range, so the label on your bottle is the authority. At 64% or more the whisky is much stronger than a standard 40% bottle, and a few drops of water make it easier to taste. If you want a low price, look for the launch pricing in Canada or the United States, and be careful with resale prices, because a limited cask strength release can sell far above what it first cost.

Alberta Distillers Ltd and the Brand

The Whiskey Wash says the distillery was founded in 1946 and is owned by Beam Suntory. The Gate names George Teichroeb as general manager of Alberta Distillers Ltd and links to the distillery’s own site. Wikipedia says WhistlePig’s first sourced whiskey was 10 year old Canadian whiskey from the Alberta Premium distillery, which is why Alberta shows up in discussions of American craft rye, and our WhistlePig 18 Year review covers that story.

So what is Alberta Premium whiskey? It is a Canadian whisky brand, sometimes searched as Alberta Premium rye whisky, from that distillery in Calgary, best known for its 100% rye. The cask strength rye is one expression, and our catalogue also lists two much older bottles under the brand, a 25 Year and a 30 Year Dark Horse. We also see searches for an Alberta dark batch rye, which is a different Alberta Premium expression that we do not currently list.

How to Taste a Cask Strength Rye

A whisky at 63.5% to 66.1% needs a careful approach. Pour about half an ounce into a tulip glass and let it rest for several minutes before nosing, because a hard sniff of high-strength spirit mostly registers alcohol. Nose it gently from a distance first, then closer. The published notes suggest vanilla, caramel and dark fruit, but your own notes matter more.

Take a very small first sip and hold it on the tongue. The first taste is mostly heat, so do not judge on it. The Gate’s reviewer found the second sip brought out creamy caramel, which is a good reminder to taste more than once. Add two or three drops of water, wait a minute, and taste again, or try a single large ice cube, which The Gate found made the whisky surprisingly sweet.

Serve it away from strong food and keep a glass of water nearby. Write a line or two about each tasting, noting the water and the glass, so that you can compare it later with a lower-strength rye. Keep the notes with the bottle.

Mistakes to Avoid

A few errors come up often with a limited cask strength release:

  • Trusting a single strength figure. The reports range from 63.5% to 66.1%, so check the label.
  • Paying a resale premium without comparing. The launch price was about $70, so compare before you pay far more.
  • Assuming Canadian rye means a low-rye blend. This one is described as 100% rye.
  • Skipping the year. Each release can differ, so ask which one you are buying.
  • Over-diluting. A few drops of water are plenty.

One more point is storage. Keep bottles upright, away from heat and light, and reseal them after each pour. If you are buying as a gift, a cask strength rye suits someone who already likes strong whisky, and you should make sure the recipient is 21 or older. Keep the receipt and the order number in case a return is needed within the return window.
