Macallan 12 vs 18: Which Is Worth Buying?

By Caskworth Editorial Published July 11, 2026Updated November 7, 20269 min read

Macallan 12 vs 18 comes down to a $205 gap: the Macallan 12 Double Cask is $74.99 at Caskworth and the Macallan 18 Double Cask is $279.99. For that money you get six more years in cask, a higher bottling strength and, according to the distillery, whisky that spent its whole maturation in sherry seasoned oak. This guide compares the two on price, strength, casks and cost per pour, explains the Macallan 12 Sherry Oak that sits between them, and gives a clear answer on which to buy.

What Macallan Says About Each Bottle

Both bottles come from The Macallan’s Double Cask range, which the distillery says combines sherry seasoned American and European oak casks. On the official Macallan 12 Double Cask page, the distillery describes the 12 as matured in sherry seasoned American and European oak casks, with fudge, candied orange and lemon and vanilla with soft spice on the nose, milk chocolate raisins and creamy toffee on the palate, and a medium finish with sweet oak. It says the Double Cask collection was first launched in 2016.

For the 18, the official Macallan 18 Double Cask page says the whisky is matured for 18 years in sherry seasoned American and European oak casks, seasoned in Jerez de la Frontera, Spain. It adds that every cask in this expression spent its entire maturation exclusively in sherry seasoned oak, that the colour is entirely natural, and that the headline flavours are toffee, sweet ginger and sultanas.

Notice what the two descriptions share and where they differ. Both are sherry-led and built on the same pair of oak types. The 18 puts the sherry seasoning and the age statement front and centre, while the 12 leans on fudge, citrus peel and chocolate. Those are the distillery’s own words, so treat them as a guide to style, not a score.

Macallan 18 vs 12: What the Extra $205 Buys

Comparing Macallan 18 vs 12 in plain terms, the extra $205 buys three things you can check on the label and the listing:

  • Six more years of maturation. That is the biggest difference, and it is the reason the price is higher. Between 12 and 18 each extra year costs about $34, which is a modest premium for age compared with older Macallan bottles.
  • A higher strength. The 18 is bottled at 43%, against 40% for the 12 Double Cask. Three points more alcohol usually gives a fuller, rounder feel in the mouth.
  • A stricter cask story. The distillery says every cask in the 18 spent its entire maturation in sherry seasoned oak. That is a clear, checkable statement about how the whisky was made.

What the money does not buy is a guarantee that you will prefer it. Age adds depth and softness, but some drinkers like the brighter fruit and fudge of the 12. The reason older bottles cost so much more is straightforward. Whisky maturing in cask loses a little to evaporation every year, the so-called angel’s share, so a cask that waits 18 years yields fewer bottles than one that waits 12, and the money tied up in warehouse stock for six extra years has to be recovered. That is also why the difference between macallan 12 and 18 shows up first in the price and only second in the glass. If you are choosing between the two for the first time, our wider Macallan price guide by age shows where each bottle sits on the full ladder from 12 to 40 years.

Is Macallan 12 or 18 Better for You?

People searching macallan 12 or 18 usually want a verdict, and the honest answer depends on how you drink. Use this checklist:

  • You mix it or add ice: buy the 12. Spending $280 on a bottle you will dilute wastes the age.
  • You drink it neat or with a few drops of water: the 18 gives you more to notice, and the higher strength holds up to a splash of water.
  • It is a gift for someone who knows whisky: the 18, because the age statement is easy to recognise and the price signals care.
  • It is a gift for a newcomer: the 12, which is easier to enjoy and leaves money for a second bottle.
  • You want to learn the range: buy both and taste them side by side. The pair costs about $355 together and clears our $249.99 minimum order on its own.

If you are asking is macallan 12 or 18 better in absolute terms, most reviewers rate the 18 as the more complete whisky, but they split on whether the extra cost is justified. One independent reviewer, Luke Rymarz, compares the two in his Whisky Video 25: Macallan 12 vs 18. It is a single opinion, so use it as one view alongside your own tasting.

Which Macallan 12 Is Best: Double Cask or Sherry Oak?

Many buyers also ask which Macallan 12 is best, because Macallan sells two 12 year old styles. Our catalog lists both:

  • Macallan 12 Double Cask, $74.99, 40%. Our listing describes it as matured in a combination of American and European oak sherry-seasoned casks, warm, rich and fruity, and calls it the perfect introduction to The Macallan. You can buy the Macallan 12 Double Cask here.
  • Macallan 12 Sherry Oak, $79.99, 43%. A step up in strength for $5 more. If you like a more traditional sherried style and a fuller feel, the Macallan 12 Sherry Oak is the better 12 for slow sipping.

For most people the Double Cask is the better first bottle, because it is the cheaper of the two and a good guide to the house style. If you already know you like sherried whisky, the extra $5 and three points of strength make the Sherry Oak the better value per pour. The macallan sherry oak 12 year price is therefore a small premium over the Double Cask, and the difference is mostly strength.

How to Taste Them Side by Side

A comparison tasting is the cheapest way to settle the question, and it works best when you control the variables:

  1. Use the same glass for both. A tulip-shaped glass concentrates aroma. Rinse it between pours.
  2. Pour equal measures. Half an ounce each is plenty.
  3. Start with the 12, then the 18. Going from lighter to richer stops the 18 from making the 12 taste thin.
  4. Nose before you sip. Note whether you smell more fruit and fudge or more dried fruit and ginger.
  5. Add two or three drops of water to each. The 40% bottle will change little, and the 43% bottle may open slightly.
  6. Wait a minute between the two. A sip of water and a plain cracker reset your palate.

Write down one sentence for each whisky. After a few minutes you will know whether the 18 is a different drink for you, or just a more expensive version of the 12. Either answer is useful, and both save you from buying the wrong bottle.
