What Is Courvoisier Cognac?

Wikipedia's Courvoisier article says Emmanuel Courvoisier started the company in 1796 in Bercy, a suburb of Paris, with Louis Gallois, and that Felix Courvoisier and Jules Gallois moved it to Jarnac in 1828, although the infobox gives 1835, so the article is not consistent. It says Allied Domecq owned the brand in the 1990s, then Beam Global and later Suntory Global Spirits, and that Campari Group completed its acquisition on 1 May 2024. So anyone who still reads that Courvoisier belongs to Beam Suntory is reading dated information.

The article adds that blends use eaux-de-vie from Grande Champagne, Petite Champagne, Borderies and Fin Bois, and calls Courvoisier the most untypical of the big four cognac houses. People often ask what is Courvoisier, what is Courvoisier cognac, or whether Courvoisier brandy is cognac: it is a brandy from the cognac region made from distilled wine, and the article lists VS, VSOP Fine Cognac, VSOP Exclusif, XO, Napoleon Fine Champagne, Initiale Extra and L'Essence among its bottles. Anyone looking for the Courvoisier VS cognac official website should check the house's own pages for current products, because ranges change.

What Is Courvoisier VSOP? The Age Grades

Courvoisier VSOP is a cognac made from brandies aged at least four years. Wikipedia's Cognac article lists the grades set by the cognac board: VS is the youngest brandy aged at least two years, VSOP at least four, Napoleon at least six, XO at least ten since a rule change in April 2018, and XXO at least fourteen. So a Courvoisier VSOP sits between the VS and the XO. Our Courvoisier VSOP is $49.99, and a search for Courvoisier VSOP price lands on it.

The practical split is use: a VS for mixing and highballs, a VSOP for stirred drinks and light sipping, and an XO for sipping. Our cognac cocktails guide works out the cognac cost of a Sidecar for each grade.

Courvoisier Cognac Price Check

The Courvoisier cognac price depends on the grade, and published sources disagree. Tastings.com listed the XO at $200 and the VSOP at $42 in a 2025 review, while a price tracker gave $114.99 to $149.99 for the XO and $35.99 to $39.99 for the VSOP. Neither is current, so treat them as a rough guide: the XO tends to cost three to five times the VSOP. Our $179.99 XO and $49.99 VSOP fit that ratio, at about 3.6 times.

The price of cognac Courvoisier bottles also varies by market and by tax, so compare like with like. For a spoken review of the XO, Bespoke Unit's Courvoisier X.O. Cognac Review is a handy companion, and reviews put the XO in the low 90s on score sites. Treat scores as one opinion.

Which Courvoisier for Cocktails, Sipping and Gifts

For cocktails, the VS at $34.99 is enough: it costs about $2.33 of cognac in each Sidecar, using the IBA's 50 ml measure, and the VSOP at $49.99 adds only about $1.00 more per drink. For sipping, the VSOP and the XO reward the extra spend, and the 12 Year at $89.99 is the age-stated bridge between them. For a gift, the XO at $179.99 is the usual choice, because the grade is widely recognised and the bottle looks the part.

The older and rarer bottles are different. Our catalogue lists the 21 Year at $299.99, the Succession J.S. at $1,499.99 and L'Essence at $3,999.99, and we have no verified detail on them beyond the catalogue price and the Wikipedia range list, so we do not describe their flavour. If one of them interests you, ask us to confirm the details before ordering, and check the label for the exact edition.

A simple rule is to buy for the use, not the label: a bottle you will mix does not need to be an XO, and a bottle you will sip is worth a step up from the VS. Whichever you choose, keep the bottle upright and sealed away from heat and light, and keep the receipt and order number in case you need to return an unopened bottle within the return window. If the bottle is a gift, make sure the recipient is 21 or older and enjoys cognac, because an XO is not a casual present for someone who prefers other spirits.

How to Compare VS, VSOP and XO

The best way to learn the grades is a three-glass flight. Pour half an ounce each of the Courvoisier VS, VSOP and XO, which together cost $264.97, into tulip glasses at room temperature, and taste them from the youngest to the oldest. Nose each gently, take a small first sip and let it coat the mouth, and add a drop of water to each. Write one line per glass about weight, sweetness and finish.

Many drinkers find that the VS is bright and light, that the VSOP adds roundness, and that the XO is deeper and longer. That is the pattern the age grades suggest, and it is a better guide to what you prefer than any score. We have not poured these bottles ourselves for this guide, so we do not quote our own notes. Keep a glass of water nearby and keep your notes with the bottles.

Mistakes to Avoid

A few errors come up often with bottles like these:

  • Reading dated ownership. Wikipedia says Campari completed its acquisition on 1 May 2024.
  • Using an XO in a Sidecar. The depth of a ten year old cognac is lost under lemon and liqueur.
  • Trusting one price figure. Sources disagree, so compare current listings.
  • Confusing the grades. VSOP is at least four years, XO at least ten.
  • Assuming a flavoured bottle is a standard grade. Check what the Rose is before ordering.

One more point is storage. Keep bottles upright, away from heat and light, and reseal them after each pour. If you are buying as a gift, make sure the recipient is 21 or older and enjoys the style, and keep the receipt and order number in case a return is needed within the return window.
