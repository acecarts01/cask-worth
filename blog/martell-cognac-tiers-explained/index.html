Martell Cognac Tiers Explained: VS to XO

By Caskworth Editorial September 1, 20263 min readCognac Guide

Founded in 1715, Martell is the oldest of the major cognac houses — here's how to navigate its lineup from entry to prestige.

A House Built on Consistency

Martell has been producing cognac since 1715, longer than any other of the major houses, and its style leans toward a lighter, more floral profile than some competitors — a result of favoring Borderies and fine Champagne eaux-de-vie in its blends.

The Core Tiers

Martell VSOP Cognac

The house's core expression, showing the characteristic Martell lightness with dried fruit and gentle spice — a reliable, food-friendly cognac for regular drinking.

Martell Cordon Bleu

Created in 1912 and still one of the house's signature bottlings, sitting above standard XO in both age and prestige within the Martell range.

Buying Considerations

Because Martell's style is lighter and more floral than houses that lean on Grande Champagne, it tends to suit drinkers who find heavier cognacs overwhelming, or who prefer cognac in cocktails where a lighter profile doesn't get lost.

Beyond Martell

If you want to compare Martell's lighter style against other approaches, Tesseron Cognac ($139.99) and Léopold Gourmel ($289.99) both showcase more terroir-driven, Grande-Champagne-forward profiles worth tasting side by side.
