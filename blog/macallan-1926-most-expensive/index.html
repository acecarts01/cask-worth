Macallan 1926 Buy Online: The 60 Year Old Whisky, Its Auction Record and Price

By Caskworth Editorial Published July 11, 2026Updated November 7, 20269 min read

The Macallan 1926 is a 60 year old single malt that has become the world most valuable whisky: one bottle with an Adami label sold at Sotheby’s in November 2023 for £2,187,500, and Caskworth lists the Fine & Rare 60 Year at $980,000. If you are researching a macallan 1926 buy online, this guide sets out what is documented about the release, how auction prices rose, how to read a listing for a bottle at this level, and which more attainable Macallan rarities are in our range.

What the Macallan 1926 Is

Sotheby’s, which has sold several of these bottles, describes the 1926 Macallan as a whisky matured in a single sherry cask for six decades. Forty bottles were produced, and they were bottled in 1986. Our catalogue lists the Macallan Fine & Rare 1926 at 43% ABV, while the link address of the Sotheby’s article gives 42.8%, so confirm the exact strength for any specific bottle. You can read the auction house’s account in Sotheby’s article on the Macallan Valerio Adami 60 Year Old.

The key facts are few, which is part of the appeal. One cask, 60 years, 40 bottles. There is no regular release to compare it with, and nothing that can be made again, because the barrel that produced it is gone. Whisky also stops maturing once it is bottled, so the whisky in these bottles is still 60 years old however long they sit, which is why condition, fill level and provenance matter more than any further ageing.

The Three Label Types

The 40 bottles were not all dressed the same way, and the label is a large part of a bottle’s identity and value. According to Sotheby’s:

  • 12 bottles with labels by Pop Artist Sir Peter Blake, dated 1986.
  • 12 bottles with labels by Italian painter Valerio Adami, dated 1993.
  • Up to 14 bottles with the Fine & Rare label, and 2 bottles with no labels.

That is why searches such as macallan 1926 peter blake and macallan 1926 valerio adami are common: the artist label identifies which of the 40 a bottle is. The Caskworth listing is the Fine & Rare version, so it falls in the group of up to 14 plain-label bottles. Anyone planning a macallan fine rare buy should confirm which version a listing refers to, because the labels carry different values.

Auction History: How the Price Climbed

Sotheby’s traces the bottles’ auction history, and it shows how a rare-whisky market can move:

The 2023 sale, on 18 November, is the one that made headlines. Sotheby’s says the Adami bottle sold for 2,187,500 pounds, nearly twice its high estimate of £1.2 million, and describes it as the most expensive whisky ever sold at auction. Whisky Advocate reports the winning bid as £1.75 million, and the total including the buyer’s premium as $2,724,908, in its report on the sale. Reading the two together, the £2,187,500 figure is the all-in price. For a short film of the bottle, Sotheby’s has The World’s Most Valuable Whisky: The Macallan Adami 1926.

The climb from £5,000 in 1987 to more than £2 million in 2023 is about a 440-fold increase in 36 years. Our guide to the most expensive whisky auction records puts these sales in context.

Why It Is the Most Expensive Single Malt

Four things combine to make this the most expensive single malt in the world, and each is documented:

  • Extreme scarcity. Forty bottles from one cask, and the cask cannot be refilled.
  • Age. Sixty years in a single sherry cask is far beyond the age statement of almost any bottle on the market.
  • Artists’ labels. The Blake and Adami labels, by well-known artists, make some bottles art objects as well as whisky.
  • Auction record. Each record sale raised the bar, drawing collectors who see the bottle as a trophy asset.

That is why anyone asking about the world most valuable whisky ends up here. It is a collector’s asset, and its price tracks collector demand, not the cost of making whisky. If you are considering rare whisky as an asset, read our rare whisky investment guide first, and remember that prices at auction can fall as well as rise.

How Auction Premiums Change the Headline Price

The headline price of an auction result is not always the winning bid. At the 2023 Sotheby’s sale, Whisky Advocate reports a winning bid of £1.75 million, and the total of £2,187,500 includes the buyer’s premium. Dividing one by the other shows a premium of 25% on top of the winning bid. That is why the same sale appears in different articles as £1.75 million, £2.19 million or about $2.7 million.

When you compare a private listing with an auction result, compare like with like. A private sale price is usually all-in, while an auction headline may or may not include the premium and fees. It also matters which version the bottle is: the 2019 record of £1,452,000 was for a Fine & Rare bottle, and the 2023 record was for an Adami bottle, so the two are not direct comparisons.

Exchange rates add another layer. The $2.7 million figure is a conversion at the time of the sale, so it will not match the amount a buyer paying in dollars would see today. If you are tracking prices as an investment, record the currency, the date and whether premium was included.

Storage and insurance deserve a plan before the bottle arrives. Keep it upright, in its box if it has one, handled as little as possible, away from direct light and temperature swings, and in a place where it will not be knocked. Ask your insurer to list the bottle at a documented value, and keep the invoice, any certificate and photographs of the label and the fill level. At this level of value those records matter as much as the whisky, because they are what a future buyer or an insurer will want to see.
