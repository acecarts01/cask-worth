Buy Scotch After Tariff Removal: What Changed in 2026 for Scotch, Cognac and Other Spirits

By Caskworth Editorial Published September 8, 2026Updated November 9, 20269 min read

If you want to buy Scotch after tariff removal, the facts are these: the US tariff on Scotch whisky ended on 24 July 2026, cognac was still facing a 10% tariff as of mid-October 2026, and shelf prices do not always move when a tariff does. This guide sets out what changed and when, using dated reporting from The Whiskey Wash, The Spirits Business and Vinetur, explains why savings reach shoppers slowly, covers what happened to Irish whiskey and Canadian whisky, and shows how to compare current Scotch and cognac prices at Caskworth. Trade rules change often, so treat this as a dated summary and check official notices.

What Changed for Scotch on 24 July 2026

The Whiskey Wash reports that the tariff on Scotch whisky entering the United States lifted at 12.01am Eastern on 24 July 2026. It says the levy had been a 10% tariff on UK goods applied from April 2025, which also covered whisky, and that the wider 10% tariff on UK goods remains with whisky carved out as a category exemption. You can read the explainer in The Scotch Tariff Is Gone: Everything You Need to Know.

The Spirits Business confirms the date, saying the tariff was officially removed on 24 July, and quotes the Scotch Whisky Association’s Ian Duddy saying the removal provides greater confidence to invest, grow exports and support jobs. Its report, “Good day for Scotland”: US lifts tariffs on Scotch, also notes that Pernod Ricard hopes for the removal of tariffs on other categories including Irish whiskey, Champagne and cognac.

So searches for scotch whisky tariffs 2026 have a clear answer: for Scotch, the tariff ended in July.

Why Scotch Is Not Instantly Cheaper

People ask why is scotch cheaper now, and the honest answer is that it may not be yet. The Whiskey Wash makes four points that explain it:

  • The tariff was charged on the importer’s landed customs value, not the shelf price. Its example is a $60 bottle, where the tariff added a few dollars rather than the six dollars a 10% shelf-price tax would imply.
  • Stock already imported with duty paid will sell at tariff-era prices.
  • Importers and distributors absorbed part of the tariff, so they have little reason to pass the whole saving on.
  • No date is given for shelf prices to fall. The article expects Scotch to get cheaper a little, probably slowly, and probably less than the headline number suggests.

For a short broadcast explainer on how tariffs and taxes reach the whiskey business, see CNBC’s How tariffs and taxes impact the whiskey business. That is a useful reality check. A removed tariff is not a sale. The sensible approach is to compare actual list prices today, not to wait for a price drop that may be small, and to remember that these are the writer’s expectations, not a forecast we can promise. We do not claim any Caskworth price has fallen because of the tariff; check the product page for the current price of any bottle.

Cognac: Still Tariffed as of October 2026

Cognac did not get the same treatment. Vinetur reported on 13 October 2026 that the Distilled Spirits Council of the United States (DISCUS) was urging the White House to lift a 10% tariff on French cognac and certain European liqueurs, and that the White House had not issued a public response as of that Monday. The report says some other European spirits have already received tariff relief, without naming which. You can read it at Vinetur.

For buyers asking about us tariffs on cognac or a cognac price increase 2026, that means the picture is unsettled. A tariff on a cognac that is already in a US warehouse does not change its price until new stock arrives, and a future change could go either way. We cannot predict it. What you can do is compare the current list prices below and decide whether a bottle is worth it to you now.

Irish Whiskey and Canadian Whisky

Two other categories are in the news. On Irish whiskey, reporting carried by Fox stations said that at the end of a visit to Ireland on Sunday 13 September 2026, President Trump said he intends to remove a 10% tariff on Irish whiskey. The report gave no effective date, and we have not found an official notice, so check the US Trade Representative or the Federal Register for the current position.

On Canadian whisky, The Whiskey Wash reports that a 50% duty on Canadian whisky takes effect on 19 August 2026. We have not verified how that duty is being applied, so if you are buying Canadian whisky, check the product page for current pricing and availability. These are examples of how quickly spirits trade rules move: three categories, three different situations, all within a few months.

A Worked Example of How a Tariff Reaches the Shelf

The Whiskey Wash explains that a tariff is charged on the importer’s landed customs value, not the shelf price. To see why that matters, take a simple illustration, which is arithmetic and not a figure from any producer. Suppose a bottle with a $60 shelf price had a landed customs value of $30. A 10% tariff on that value is $3, which is far less than the $6 a 10% tax on the shelf price would imply.

That is why a tariff cut does not translate into a matching shelf-price cut. The saving starts as a few dollars per bottle at the importer, and then it is shared between importer, distributor and retailer, with each deciding how much to pass on. If duty-paid stock is already on the shelf, the saving only reaches new stock. For a shopper, the lesson is simple: judge each bottle on its current price and your own taste, and treat any promised savings with caution until you see the price on the page.

The same logic applies to cognac in reverse. A new tariff or a higher one affects new shipments first, so existing stock may carry the older price for some time. Neither direction is predictable from outside the trade.
