Camus Cognac XO: Elegance vs Borderies, the Full Range and What the Borderies Cru Is

By Caskworth Editorial Published September 6, 2026Updated November 9, 20269 min read

Camus cognac XO comes in two lines in our catalogue: the Camus XO Elegance at $189.99 and the Camus XO Borderies at $249.99, which our catalogue describes as 100% Borderies eaux-de-vie. Wikipedia says the house was founded in 1863 by Jean-Baptiste Camus and is run by the fifth generation of the family, with Cyril Camus at its head, and that its Borderies XO, first released in 2000, is the flagship. This guide explains the Borderies cru, compares the five Camus bottles we list from $39.99 to $499.99 with price per pour, and says which to buy first.

What Is Camus Cognac?

Wikipedia's Camus Cognac article says Jean-Baptiste Camus founded the brand in 1863 and that it is headquartered in Cognac, France. Five generations of the Camus family have run the company, and Cyril Camus, the fifth generation, heads it. The article says the company has about 500 employees in eight countries and that Camus is sold in most countries and in almost every international airport. It also carries maintenance notes saying it needs more citations, so we treat its claims as a starting point.

The family's base is in the Borderies growing area, where it expanded its vineyards in 1991. The article says Cyril Camus developed a Borderies XO blend from the family's own vineyards, first released in 2000 and described as the flagship, and that he also developed a cognac from the Ile de Re. We do not repeat the claim, found in older guides, that Camus is the largest independent family-owned cognac house, because the article does not say it. People searching for cognac Camus usually want one of the bottles below. Our cognac cocktails guide shows how a VS or VSOP is used in drinks.

The Borderies Cru

Wikipedia’s Borderies article says Borderies is the smallest of the cognac crus, at 4,160 hectares (10,300 acres), with soils of clay and flint stones formed from the breakdown of limestone. That is the verified part. Our catalogue describes the Camus XO Borderies as 100% Borderies eaux-de-vie with violet floral notes and calls the terroir the rarest in cognac, but those are catalogue descriptions, and the article gives no flavour profile for the cru, so we cannot confirm them.

The practical point is the one the family makes: a bottle drawn from one cru shows that area more clearly than a blend across several. Camus Borderies XO is the example, and the family's vineyards in the area are the reason the house makes it. If you want to taste the difference, compare a Borderies bottle with the Elegance bottle at the same grade.

Camus XO Elegance vs XO Borderies

The Camus XO Elegance at $189.99 and the Camus XO Borderies at $249.99 are both XO grade, so both are made from brandies at least ten years old under the cognac age grades Wikipedia lists. The difference is the source: the Elegance line is a blend described in our catalogue as dried fruit, intense floral and warm spice, while the Borderies is described as single-cru. The $60 gap is the price of that difference.

US retail listings for the Borderies XO that we saw ranged from about $210 to $300 for a 750 ml bottle, so our $249.99 sits inside that range. Several listings were for a separate Family Reserve bottling, so check which product you are buying. Retail pages are marketing copy, so we do not quote their tasting notes, and we have not poured the bottles ourselves. Reading VS, VSOP and XO

Wikipedia's Cognac article lists the age grades set by the cognac board: VS is the youngest brandy aged at least two years, VSOP at least four, Napoleon at least six, XO at least ten since a rule change in April 2018, and XXO at least fourteen. So a Camus Napoleon sits between the VSOP and the XO, and we do not currently list one. Searches for a Camus Napoleon cognac or a Camus XO superior lead to products that we do not stock, so check the label for the exact grade.

The grades set a minimum, not a style, and houses blend well above them. Our catalogue describes the VSOP as aged 4 to 10 years, which is consistent with a four year minimum. For everyday mixing a VS is enough, for sipping a VSOP or XO rewards the extra spend, and for a gift an XO in a presentation box is the usual choice.

How to Taste Cognac

Cognac rewards a slow approach. Use a tulip glass or a small snifter, pour about an ounce, and hold the glass at room temperature for a minute before nosing. Warming the glass in your hands is unnecessary and can push alcohol forward, so nose gently from a distance first, then closer. Take a small first sip and let it coat the mouth before swallowing.

A good test of any house is to taste the VS, VSOP and XO side by side. Pour half an ounce of each, starting with the youngest, and write one line per glass about weight, sweetness and finish. The catalogue descriptions give a vocabulary to look for, such as floral, light fruit, dried fruit and spice, but your own notes matter more, and we have not poured these bottles ourselves.

To compare the Elegance and Borderies lines, taste the two XO bottles one after the other with a glass of water between them. If the single-cru bottle feels different to you, the extra $60 may be worth it. If not, the Elegance saves money. Either way, keep a note so that the next purchase is a choice, not a guess.

Mistakes to Avoid

A few errors come up often with bottles like these:

  • Confusing the Borderies XO with the Family Reserve. Retail listings mix them up, so check the label and the product name.
  • Assuming XO means one style. The Elegance and Borderies lines differ in source.
  • Trusting unverified history. Claims such as the largest independent house are not in the Wikipedia article.
  • Using an XO in cocktails. A VS or VSOP is better value for mixing.
  • Skipping the price check. US listings for the Borderies XO vary by about $90.

One more point is storage. Keep bottles upright, away from heat and light, and reseal them after each pour. If you are buying as a gift, make sure the recipient is 21 or older and enjoys the style, and keep the receipt and order number in case a return is needed within the return window.
