Bottled-in-Bond Explained: America's Oldest Consumer Protection Law

By Caskworth Editorial August 27, 20265 min readBourbon Education

Bottled-in-Bond is the oldest consumer protection label in American spirits — a 19th-century law that still tells you exactly what's in the bottle today.

The 1897 Law That Still Matters

The Bottled-in-Bond Act was signed in 1897, decades before the FDA existed, in response to widespread whiskey adulteration — producers cutting spirit with anything from tobacco spit to iodine to fake the color and bite of aged whiskey. Congress created a federal guarantee: if a distillery followed the rules, the government would certify what was in the bottle.

What the Label Guarantees

To carry the Bottled-in-Bond label today, a whiskey must be the product of one distillation season at one distillery by one distiller, aged at least four years in a federally bonded warehouse under U.S. government supervision, and bottled at exactly 100 proof (50% ABV). It's one of the only labeling terms in American whiskey with real legal teeth behind it.
