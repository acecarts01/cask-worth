Pappy Van Winkle 23 Year Review: Taste, Price and Whether It Is Worth It

Pappy Van Winkle 23 Year Review: What the Bottle Is

The Pappy Van Winkle 23 Year is a Kentucky straight bourbon from the Van Winkle family and Buffalo Trace Distillery. The distillery’s own Pappy Van Winkle 23 Year page lists it at 95.6 proof (47.8% ABV), made with a wheated mash, and says each barrel matures for 23 years. It also says the barrels are carefully selected from the heart of the warehouse and calls the bourbon exceptionally rare.

“Wheated” means wheat replaces rye as the secondary grain, which usually gives a softer, rounder bourbon. Buffalo Trace does not publish exact grain percentages, so any figure you see online is not the distillery’s. The family’s own story, from the Stitzel-Weller Distillery that Pappy opened in 1935 to the joint venture with Buffalo Trace in 2002, is told on the Buffalo Trace Van Winkle page.

The 23 Year is the oldest of the three standard Pappy bottlings (15, 20 and 23), which is why people searching for the oldest pappy van winkle buy usually end up on this bottle.

The Van Winkle Family Behind the Name

The brand page says the Van Winkle family’s involvement in bourbon began in the late 1800s, that Pappy opened the Stitzel-Weller Distillery in Louisville in 1935, and that his son Julian Jr. carried on the legacy until 1972, when the distillery was sold. It then describes the 2002 joint venture with Buffalo Trace, and the family’s continued focus on high-quality wheated bourbon.

That history matters to buyers for one reason: it is a large part of why the name carries a premium. Julian Van Winkle tells the family story himself in Pappy Van Winkle 23-Year Bourbon and Family History with Julian Van Winkle, a video from Pappy & Company. It is a brand-produced video, so treat it as the family’s own account rather than an independent review.

The wider range includes bottles at other ages. Our catalogue lists the Old Rip Van Winkle 10 Year at $995 and 45% ABV, and the 15 Year at 53.5% ABV, so the three Pappy bottlings sit above a 10 year entry point rather than standing alone.

Tasting Notes From the Distillery

Because this is a review of a bottle most drinkers will never pour, the most useful thing we can do is relay what the producer publishes and read it carefully. Buffalo Trace gives one combined note: caramel, ripe apples, cherries, oak and tobacco with a hint of chocolate. It describes the finish as pronounced wood flavours leading to a sweet caramel finish that is long-lasting, and it suggests serving the bourbon neat.

What that tells you in practice:

  • Wood leads. After 23 years in charred oak the barrel character is prominent, which is why the notes include oak and tobacco, not just fruit.
  • Sweetness is caramel, not sugar. The finish is described as sweet caramel, which is what wheat usually contributes.
  • Fruit is dark and baked. Ripe apples and cherries suggest fruit that has been cooked down, not fresh.
  • It is a sipping bourbon. The neat serve suggestion is the distillery’s own.

The distillery page also lists awards, from 2018 and 2019 competitions. Treat awards as one data point: they say the bourbon is well made, not that it will suit your taste.

Pappy 23 vs 20: Is the Upgrade Worth It?

The Pappy Van Winkle 20 Year Family Reserve is $2,999.99 at 45.2% ABV. The 23 costs $1,500 more, which is a 50% premium, and it is bottled at a slightly higher strength (47.8%). For three more years in the barrel you get more wood influence and a longer finish, according to the distillery’s notes. Whether that is worth $1,500 depends on what you want:

  • Choose the 20 Year if you want the Family Reserve experience at a lower price and prefer a softer, less oak-forward profile.
  • Choose the 23 Year if you want the oldest standard release and are happy to pay for scarcity.
  • Buy both if you want to taste the difference side by side. The two make an instructive pair.

If you are still deciding, our Pappy Van Winkle 20 Year review covers the cheaper bottle in the same detail.

Why Pappy Is So Rare

Allocated pappy for sale is hard to find because demand far exceeds supply. Buffalo Trace calls the 23 Year exceptionally rare and limited-edition, and it ages each barrel for 23 years, which means the supply decisions were made more than two decades ago. Long ageing also reduces the number of usable barrels, which keeps volumes small. The result is a bourbon that is sold through allocation, and that most drinkers see only as a secondary-market listing.

People who want the rarest pappy buy online should understand that rarity and risk go together. Caskworth lists the 23 Year with the rest of our allocated and rare whiskey collection. The rarest items also carry the highest counterfeit risk, which is the next section.

How to Buy Pappy 23 Safely

High-value bourbon is a target for fakes, so apply these checks to any purchase, including ours:

  1. Confirm the exact edition. Catalogue pages for Pappy list the Family Reserve and other 23 year items separately. Read each listing carefully and compare it with the distillery’s page for proof, age and style.
  2. Compare the strength. The Family Reserve is listed at 47.8% ABV. A bottle that claims a different strength should be questioned.

How to Serve and Store It

Buffalo Trace suggests serving the 23 Year neat, and that is the best way to taste what you paid for. Use a tulip or Glencairn glass, pour a small measure at room temperature and let it sit for a few minutes. If the strength feels warm, add a few drops of water, not ice. Skip cocktails: at this price, the whiskey is the point.

Store the bottle upright, away from direct sunlight and heat, and keep the cork in good condition. An unopened bottle does not improve with time, because whiskey does not mature in the bottle, so open it when the occasion arrives instead of waiting.

Who Should Buy It

The honest verdict is that this is a bottle for a specific buyer. If you collect allocated bourbon, want the oldest standard Pappy, or are marking a major occasion, the 23 Year is the obvious choice. If you want great bourbon at a sensible price, spend the money elsewhere: our bourbon collection has well-reviewed bottles at a small fraction of the cost.

Common mistakes to avoid before you spend four figures on a bourbon:

  • Buying on the name alone. Read the distillery notes first, so you know the bourbon is oak-forward and made to be sipped neat.
  • Assuming older always means better. The 15 Year is bottled at a higher strength (53.5% ABV) and costs far less, so compare before you decide.
  • Skipping the edition check. Confirm the exact bottling, strength and price on the product page before you pay.
  • Waiting too long to open it. The whiskey stops ageing once bottled, so there is no reward for holding it back indefinitely.
