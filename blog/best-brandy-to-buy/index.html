What Counts as Brandy?

Brandy is a spirit distilled from fermented fruit, and most of it is made from grapes. In United States law, brandy is spirits distilled from the fermented juice, mash or wine of fruit, or from the residue of those, at less than 190 proof, and bottled at no less than 80 proof (40% alcohol by volume). The rule also says grape brandy aged at least two years in oak may be labelled “grape brandy” or simply “brandy”, while grape brandy aged less than two years in oak must be labelled “immature”. The text is in 27 CFR 5.145.

That definition explains why the category is so broad. Cognac, Armagnac, Spanish brandy, South African brandy and American brandy are all brandy. Calvados and apple brandy are fruit brandies made from apples. Pisco and grappa belong to the family too. What changes between them is the grape, the still, the cask and the local rules.

Some styles have stricter rules than the general definition. For cognac, the Cognac trade body (the BNIC) says the spirit must be aged at least two years in oak, and sets age grades: V.S at least 2 years, V.S.O.P at least 4 and X.O at least 10. It also says the age on the label is that of the youngest eau-de-vie in the blend. You can read the rules on the BNIC protected origin page. Our guide to cognac vs brandy covers the difference in detail.

Brandy From France: Cognac, Armagnac and Calvados

If your shortlist is brandy from France, there are three families to know, and each has its own character and price range.

  • Cognac. The best-known French brandy, made in the Cognac region under the BNIC rules. Our cognac range starts under $25 and reaches the thousands for ultra-aged blends.
  • Armagnac brandy. Made in south-west France and sold with its own age labels. Our Armagnac range starts at $49.99 for the Janneau V.S.O.P and reaches into the hundreds. For a bottle with a clear age statement at a moderate price, the Delord 15 Year Armagnac is $79.99. Our Armagnac guide explains the style in more detail.
  • Calvados. Apple brandy from Normandy. A V.S.O.P such as Christian Drouin Calvados V.S.O.P costs $44.99 and is a good first step into apple brandy.

Armagnac’s own trade body, the BNIA, says the spirit is made in south-west France and publishes age categories including V.S, V.S.O.P, Hors d’Âge and X.O. We do not quote minimum ages for those categories here, so check the label and our listing for the specific bottle before relying on a category name.

A quick comparison helps. Cognac is the widest and most consistent range, with a bottle at nearly every price. Armagnac can offer older bottles at lower prices than cognac, for example the 15 year Delord at $79.99 against $89.99 for the 12 year Courvoisier in our range. Calvados is the choice if you prefer an apple style over a grape one.

Best Brandy to Drink: Neat, Over Ice or Mixed

The best brandy to drink depends on how you plan to serve it. A practical split:

  • Neat or with a few drops of water: choose an aged bottle. A Torres 15 or 20, a V.S.O.P or X.O cognac, or an older Armagnac suits slow sipping.
  • Over ice: a V.S.O.P cognac or a South African brandy is a good fit.
  • In cocktails: use an affordable V.S or American brandy. Spending on a rare bottle for a mixed drink wastes the age.
  • After dinner: an Armagnac or a Calvados gives a different flavour profile from cognac and works well on its own.

For a short visual primer on the main families, Bartending Pro’s Cognac, Brandy and Armagnac: Alcohol 101 video runs under four minutes. It is an educator’s overview, so use it as an introduction before you read the details on each label.

How to Choose the Best Brandy for You

Work through these steps:

  1. Decide how you will drink it. Sipping calls for age. Mixing does not.
  2. Set a budget band. Under $30 gets you good everyday bottles. $30 to $80 gets you aged Spanish, entry cognac and Armagnac. Above $100 is X.O cognac and older Armagnac.
  3. Pick a style. Grape brandy such as cognac or Spanish brandy is the standard. Calvados is apple. Pisco and grappa are different again.
  4. Check the label. Look for the age statement, the strength and the origin.

If you are comparing the top brandy brands, remember that fame and quality are different things. Large houses give consistency. Smaller producers, especially in Armagnac and craft American brandy, can offer more individual bottles. Our brandy range includes both so you can taste your way across styles.

Common Mistakes

  • Assuming brandy means cognac. Cognac is one kind of brandy, with its own rules. Spanish, South African and American brandy are different styles with different prices.
  • Buying the oldest bottle you can afford. Age changes the style, and some drinkers prefer a younger, brighter brandy.
  • Ignoring the strength. A 38% bottle will taste lighter than a 43% one.
  • Using expensive brandy in mixed drinks. Save aged bottles for sipping.
  • Skipping the label. The age, origin and strength tell you more than the brand name.

Match the style to how you drink, the bottle to your budget, and you will land on the best brandy for you, not the one a ranking told you to buy.

Best Brandy FAQ

There is no single best brandy, because it depends on style and budget. For value, the Torres 15 Year at $34.99 or Klipdrift Premium at $24.99 are strong choices. For a classic, try a V.S.O.P cognac. For a special occasion, look at an aged Armagnac or an X.O cognac.
Brandy is made by distilling fermented fruit juice, mash or wine, and most of it is made from grapes. Under US rules it is bottled at no less than 40% ABV. Apple brandy, such as Calvados, uses apples, and grappa uses the pressed skins left after winemaking.
Cognac is a brandy made in the Cognac region of France under strict rules, including at least two years in oak. All cognac is brandy, but most brandy is not cognac. Spanish, South African, American and Armagnac brandies all follow different rules.

Neither is better. Cognac is usually more consistent and widely available. Armagnac can offer older bottles at lower prices, for example the Delord 15 Year at $79.99 against $89.99 for the Courvoisier 12 Year in our range. Try one of each to see which style you prefer.
