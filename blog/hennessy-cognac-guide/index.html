What V.S, V.S.O.P and X.O Mean

Is Hennessy a cognac or a brandy? Both, in a sense: cognac is a protected kind of brandy from a delimited area of western France. People searching to find out whether Hennessy is a brandy or Hennessy is brandy are usually asking this, and the answer is that Hennessy is a cognac, which is a type of brandy.

The letters on the label are age grades set by the Cognac trade body, the BNIC. On its protected origin page, the BNIC says the age of a cognac is that of its youngest eau-de-vie, and that V.S means at least 2 years, V.S.O.P at least 4 years and X.O at least 10 years. These are minimums, so a house can and usually does blend older spirits above the floor.

Hennessy VS and V.S.O.P: The Everyday Range

Hennessy V.S is the bottle most people know. Hennessy describes Hennessy Very Special as one of the most popular cognacs in the world, matured in new oak barrels, with toasted and fruit notes and a rich, clearly defined palate. The house calls it versatile and ideal for cocktails, from classic recipes to simple mixed drinks, so the Hennessy VS is the one to buy if you mostly mix.

Hennessy V.S.O.P Privilege is a step up in age and structure. On its official Hennessy V.S.O.P page, the house says the V.S.O.P was commissioned in 1817 by the future King George IV of England, and was the first achievement of Hennessy’s original Master Blender, Jean Fillioux, whose family has continued to perfect it for eight generations. Hennessy describes a nose of sweet apricot and a palate of vanilla and mellow oak.

The V.S.O.P is the usual pick for drinkers who want something smoother than the V.S for sipping but not the price of an X.O. If you are comparing it with another house, our guide to cognac VS, VSOP and XO explains how the grades work across brands.

Hennessy XO and Master Blender's Selection No. 5

Hennessy X.O at $199.99 is the classic step up. The grade means the youngest eau-de-vie in the blend is at least 10 years old, and the house sells it as its flagship. Our Hennessy XO price guide covers its price, history and tasting notes in detail. If you are deciding between Hennessy XO and a lower grade, the main differences are depth, roundness and finish rather than a different style.

Between the V.S.O.P and the X.O in price sits the Master Blender’s Selection No. 5 at $89.99 and 43% ABV. On its official page, Hennessy describes the Master Blender’s Selection as a limited series in which the Master Blender is free to create a blend that, unlike other Hennessy cognacs, will not be replicated. The No. 5 is a single batch made from eaux-de-vie hand-selected by Master Blender Renaud Fillioux de Gironde from 18 family-run exclusive distillers. Because it is a limited release, check availability on the product page.

The house also releases limited editions of its core grades, and the V.S page says the V.S expresses its personality through artist partnerships and annual limited editions. Our catalogue lists several, including the V.S.O.P Privilege Limited Edition at $69.99, the X.O Limited Edition by Ridley Scott at $229.99 and the X.O Limited Edition by Marc Newson at $249.99, all at 40% ABV. They carry the same grade names as the standard bottles, so the extra cost is largely for the design and the limited run, and the product page for each one is the place to confirm what is in the box. They suit a gift or a collection, but if you simply want to drink the cognac, the standard bottle costs less, and our shop lists both side by side so you can compare.

Hennessy Paradis and the Prestige Tier

Hennessy Paradis at $999.99 is where the range turns into a collector’s conversation. The house says on its official Hennessy Paradis page that Paradis was created by Maurice Fillioux in 1979 as a tribute to symphonic music, that he compared the blend to a symphonic orchestra, and that its eaux-de-vie are aged only in very old barrels. It also describes the cognac as having a rich copper colour and a deep, persistent finish and says it is often described as a cognac lover’s cognac.

Above it in our range are two more ultra premium bottles: Hennessy Paradis Imperial at $3,499.99 and Hennessy Richard Hennessy at $4,999.99, both at 40% ABV in our catalogue. For these, read the individual product pages and the producer’s own notes before you buy. How to Serve Hennessy

The right serve depends on the grade, and the house itself gives guidance. On its Hennessy Very Special page, the house recommends the V.S for cocktails and lists drinks such as Hennessy and ginger, a pineapple mix, a berry mojito and a honey highball. It notes that the spice of ginger ale brings out the natural spice of the cognac. The V.S.O.P page points to classic cocktails too, including the Old Fashioned.

A simple rule follows from that:

  • V.S: mix it. Use it in highballs and long drinks, where its bold, fruity character carries through the mixer.
  • V.S.O.P: either. It is smooth enough to sip and structured enough for a stirred cocktail.
  • X.O and above: sip it neat, in a tulip glass, at room temperature. A few drops of water can open the aroma.

Store any bottle upright, away from heat and direct light, and keep it sealed between pours. Cognac does not continue to age in the bottle, so an unopened bottle will not improve with time, and an opened one is best finished within a reasonable period to keep the aroma fresh.

Two common mistakes are worth avoiding. The first is spending prestige money on a bottle you plan to mix: the V.S does that job for $39.99. The second is assuming a limited edition is automatically better. Judge each bottle on its own page, its strength and your own taste.

Which Hennessy Should You Buy?

Match the bottle to the occasion:

  • Cocktails and mixed drinks: Hennessy V.S at $39.99.
  • Sipping on a budget: Hennessy V.S.O.P Privilege at $59.99.
  • A step between V.S.O.P and X.O: Master Blender’s Selection No. 5 at $89.99, while stock lasts.
  • A gift or a milestone: Hennessy X.O at $199.99.
  • A collector’s bottle: Hennessy Paradis at $999.99 or above.
