Why Port Ellen and Brora Are Called Ghost Distilleries

A ghost distillery is one that has stopped making whisky but still has maturing stock. Diageo’s own Malts site says both Port Ellen and Brora closed in 1983, in its article The lost whisky distilleries of Scotland and the two who came back. It describes Brora as closing during a hard period for the industry, when demand for medium and heavily peated whisky for blending was being met by fewer distilleries.

The Scotch Whisky Association adds a rule that matters to every collector: a bottle does not keep ageing once it is bottled, as its FAQ explains. So an age on the label tells you how long the youngest whisky in the bottle spent in cask, and it will never change.

Because the casks were filled before the closure, each release draws on a finite pool, which is why people who want a ghost distillery scotch buy usually care about the exact release, the strength and the paperwork more than the brand alone.

Buy Port Ellen Online: The Islay Name

Diageo’s Malts site says Port Ellen made smoky Islay whisky from 1825, closed in 1983 and returned in 2024 after a £185 million investment. Diageo’s story Port Ellen Reborn is dated 19 March 2024, the day it reopened. Diageo’s film The Reopening of Port Ellen Distillery shows the revived site.

The Malts article says the new distillery has two pairs of copper stills, including Phoenix Stills that replicate the originals, and a Ten Part Spirit Safe that lets distillers take more cuts than a standard safe. None of that changes the whisky in an old bottle, which was made at the old distillery.

We list three aged Port Ellen bottles. The Port Ellen 32 Year 13th Release is $7,999.99 at 52.6% ABV, the Port Ellen 38 Year Annual Release is $11,999.99 at 50.4%, and the Port Ellen 1979 First Fill Sherry 40 Year is $135,000 at 52.6%. Anyone searching for a Port Ellen annual release should note that release names and numbers can look alike, so confirm the exact one.

Brora: The Highland Name

Wikipedia says the Brora site was built in 1819 and was called Clynelish until 1968, when the newer Clynelish distillery opened and the old one was renamed Brora. Production stopped in early 1983. Diageo says it reopened on 19 May 2021, the first revival of a formerly lost whisky name, and that the team studied old ledgers, sampled remaining stocks and used old tasting notes to recreate the style.

Wikipedia also notes a detail that surprises many buyers: from May 1969 to July 1973 Brora made heavily peated whisky to offset a shortage of Islay whisky, and since 1973 it has mainly made lightly peated Highland-style spirit. So the peat level in an old Brora bottle depends on when it was distilled, which is one more reason to read the label closely.

We list the Brora 40 Year Annual Release at $10,999.99 and 49.9% ABV. Buyers who want to buy Brora whisky online and search for a Brora whisky price guide will find a range, so rely on the exact bottle in front of you rather than a general figure. The same applies to anyone who types brora 40 year buy into a search box: check strength, bottling year and packaging first.

Reading a Ghost Distillery Label

Rare bottles carry more detail than everyday ones, and each detail changes what you are buying:

  • Age statement. A number such as 32, 38 or 40 is the age of the youngest whisky in the bottle, and it is fixed once bottled.
  • Vintage year. A year such as 1979 usually points to when the spirit was distilled, which is different from the bottling year. Check both.
  • Release number. Annual releases are numbered, and names can look alike across years, so confirm which one you are buying.
  • Cask type. The 1979 bottle is described as first fill sherry, which tells you the wood that held the whisky.
  • Strength. All four bottles are above 49.9% ABV, so they behave differently from a standard 40% bottle. A few drops of water will open them up.

If a listing leaves out one of these details, ask for it before you pay. A clear seller will provide it, and a vague one is a reason to step back. Keep the box, any certificate and the receipt, because paperwork helps with insurance and with any future resale.

At home, store the bottle upright, away from light and heat, and keep it sealed until you are ready to drink it. An opened bottle should be finished within a reasonable time, since air slowly changes the whisky. For a bottle at this price, many owners prefer to pour a small amount at a time with friends, which spreads the cost of the experience and lets everyone compare the Islay and Highland styles in one sitting.

Old Stock Versus New Spirit

This is the most important distinction for anyone planning to buy Port Ellen online. Port Ellen reopened in March 2024, and Brora in May 2021, but spirit made since then is young. The Scotch Whisky Association requires three years in oak before spirit can be called Scotch, and an age statement of 32 or 40 years can only come from casks filled long before the reopenings.

So bottles with these ages come from old stock. That is what makes them a finite supply, and it is also why anyone using the phrase lost distillery Scotch should check whether the label refers to old or new spirit. A newer bottle from a reopened distillery is a different product, with a different price and a different story.

We do not claim prices will rise. Rare whisky prices can fall, and a reopened distillery adds new supply of the name.
