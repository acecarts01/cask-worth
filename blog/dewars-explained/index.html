What Is Dewars White Label Scotch and the Dewar's Story

Wikipedia's Dewar’s article says the brand was created by John Dewar Sr. in 1846, that his son Thomas Dewar helped expand it, that the company built the Aberfeldy Distillery in 1896, and that Diageo sold John Dewar & Sons to Bacardi in 1998. It lists the core expressions as White Label, created in 1899, plus the 12, 15, 18 and Signature, first created in 2003, all at 40% ABV, and a 19 Champion Edition released and retired in 2021.

So Dewars white label scotch is the oldest and cheapest of the range, and a search for Dewars whiskey, the Dewar's scotch whisky or Dewar's blended scotch whisky usually leads to it. Our Dewar’s White Label is $19.99, and the question of whether Dewars is a good scotch is best answered by tasting it beside the 12 at the same strength. Our blended Scotch guide covers the big three brands, and our cheaper bottle list is in the premium blended Scotch under $60 guide.

Double Ageing: What the Process Is

Wikipedia says Dewar's pioneered marrying the whisky in oak casks, then returning the blend to oak for further ageing after blending. That is the double ageing the brand talks about: the component whiskies are aged, blended, and then aged again together. Our catalogue describes the Dewar's 12 as double-aged for 12 years, first in oak and then married in a final cask, and the 15, 18 and 25 Year the same way.

The Double Double bottles go further. Dewar's own Double Double 21 Year page says the 21 Year Old is crafted through a four-stage ageing process, finished in Oloroso sherry casks and bottled at 46% ABV, with a nose of vine fruits, honeyed malt and cocoa, a palate of tropical fruit, citrus peel, spice, dried fruits, toasted nuts and caramel, and a finish with hints of liquorice. The page does not name or explain the four stages, so we do not guess them. For a video review, Whisky Shared's Dewar’s Double Double 21 Year Old Whisky Review is a useful companion.

Dewars White Label Price and What Alcohol Content to Expect

The Dewars white label price in our catalogue is $19.99, about $0.80 a pour, and Wikipedia gives the standard range as 40% ABV, which answers the question of Dewars alcohol content. The brand's page lists the Double Double 21 at 46%, and our catalogue lists the 21, 27 and 32 Year at 46%. The Spirits Business has reported the three as 46%, 500 ml bottles, so check the size on any bottle you receive.

Prices of everyday bottles vary by market, and our figures are list prices in US dollars. For a quick comparison, the 12 at $29.99 costs about $1.20 a pour and the 18 at $69.99 costs about $2.80, so the step up from 12 to 18 costs $1.60 a pour.

It helps to decide what you want the bottle for. For mixing in a highball or a simple cocktail, the White Label or the 12 is plenty, because the extra age of the older bottles is easy to lose under soda or citrus. For sipping, the 15 and 18 reward a slow pour, and for a gift, the Double Double 21 at $149.99 is the bottle with the brand's own awards and a clear 46% strength on the label. Whichever you choose, keep it sealed and upright away from heat and light, and keep the receipt and order number in case a return is needed within the return window.

How to Compare the Dewar's Bottles

A three-glass flight shows what the double ageing adds. Pour half an ounce each of the White Label, the 12 and the 18, which together cost $119.97, into matching glasses, and taste from the youngest to the oldest. Nose each gently, take a small first sip and add a drop of water. Write one line per glass about sweetness, weight and finish.

Many drinkers find that the White Label is light, the 12 adds honey and roundness, and the 18 adds depth. That pattern fits the brand's account of double ageing, but your own notes matter more, and we have not poured these bottles ourselves for this guide. If you add the Double Double 21 at 46%, taste it last and add water carefully.

Keep a glass of water nearby, wait a minute between pours, and keep your notes with the bottles so that your next purchase is a choice, not a guess.

Mistakes to Avoid

A few errors come up often with bottles like these:

  • Assuming Dewar's is one style. The range runs from a no-age White Label to a 32 Year at 46%.
  • Guessing the Double Double process. The brand's page names four stages without explaining them, so check its own wording.
  • Ignoring strength. The Double Double bottles are 46%, the others 40%.
  • Using a 32 Year for mixing. The White Label or 12 does the job for a few dollars.
  • Paying for age you will not taste. Compare the 12 and 18 side by side first.

One more point is storage. Keep bottles upright, away from heat and light, and reseal them after each pour. If you are buying as a gift, make sure the recipient is 21 or older and enjoys the style, and keep the receipt and order number in case a return is needed within the return window.
