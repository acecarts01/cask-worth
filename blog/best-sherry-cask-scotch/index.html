What Sherry Cask Maturation Does

Sherry cask scotch whisky is whisky that has spent some or all of its maturation in oak casks that previously held sherry. Because Scotch must be matured in oak for at least three years under the Scotch Whisky Association’s rules, the cask is one of the main things a distillery can change to shape its style. Sherry casks tend to give a darker colour and richer, dried-fruit and spice flavours than casks that previously held bourbon, which is why people search for the best sherry cask whisky as a style in its own right.

Producers are specific about what they use, and it is worth reading it:

  • GlenDronach. The distillery says its 12 year old is matured in the finest Pedro Ximénez and Oloroso sherry casks, and that it has crafted single malts in the Valley of Forgue in the Highlands since 1826. See the official GlenDronach site.
  • Glenfarclas. The distillery says John Grant personally travels to one particular family-run bodega in Spain to select only the finest European oak casks that previously held Oloroso sherry. The detail is on the Glenfarclas production page.
  • Macallan. The Macallan says its Double Cask 18 Years Old is matured in sherry seasoned American and European oak casks, seasoned in Jerez de la Frontera, Spain, and that every cask in that expression spent its entire maturation in sherry seasoned oak.

For a clear explanation of sherry styles and what they mean for whisky, First Phil Whisky’s Sherry EXPLAINED for whisky lovers video is an independent primer. It is one educator’s view, so use it as background alongside the producers’ own statements above.

Aberlour A'bunadh: The Cask-Strength Option

Searches for a bunadh aberlour usually mean the cask-strength bottling, and ours is the Aberlour A’bunadh Cask Strength at $69.99 and 61.2% ABV. Our listing describes it as non-chill filtered, at cask strength, from first fill Oloroso sherry casks. Aberlour itself describes the A’bunadh as a cask-strength whisky, and states a different strength for each batch, so check the batch strength on the bottle you receive.

A bottle at 61.2% needs a little technique:

  • Pour a small measure, half an ounce is enough.
  • Add water a few drops at a time. At this strength, water opens the aroma and softens the heat.
  • Use it for sipping, not mixing. The price per pour is about $2.80, but the strength is better enjoyed slowly.

If you like a 12 year aberlour at 40%, the A’bunadh is the intense version of the same distillery. A good plan is to buy the Aberlour 12 Year Double Cask and the A’bunadh together and compare them, which is exactly the kind of order that clears our $249.99 minimum when you add a third bottle.

Because the cask-strength bottle is the most distinctive sherry cask scotch under $100, it is worth buying even if you already own a sherried 12.

Sherry Casks vs Bourbon Casks

Most Scotch is matured in ex-bourbon casks, and many bottles combine both. Our listing for the Glenfiddich 18 Year says it is matured in Oloroso sherry and bourbon casks and married in small batches, and the Aberlour 12 Year Double Cask is listed as bourbon and Oloroso sherry. These blended-cask bottles are a good middle ground if a full sherry bottle is too heavy for you.

A simple test tells you which side you are on. If you prefer lighter, fruit-led whisky, start with a bourbon-cask or double-cask bottle. If you like dessert, dark fruit and spice, go straight to GlenDronach or Glenfarclas. And if you are not sure, buy one of each and compare them next to a Speyside bourbon-cask malt such as the Glenfiddich 12, which our best Scotch under $100 guide covers.

How to Choose and Buy

Use this short path:

  1. Pick your budget. Under $50 for GlenDronach 12, Aberlour 12 or Glenfarclas 10. $50 to $100 for Glenfarclas 15 or GlenDronach 15 and 18. Above $100 for the 21 year olds and Macallan 18.
  2. Pick your intensity. Gentle is Aberlour 12 Double Cask. Deep is GlenDronach. Intense is A’bunadh.
  3. Check the strength. 43% to 48% bottles feel fuller than 40% bottles.
  4. Taste with water. A few drops help every one of these.
