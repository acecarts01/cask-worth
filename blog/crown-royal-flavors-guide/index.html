Crown Royal Chocolate: What Is Chocolate Crown Royal?

The brand's Crown Royal Chocolate page describes a silky smooth chocolate flavor blended with whisky, at 35% alcohol by volume. It lists a nose of creamy chocolate and fudge with top notes of whisky, vanilla and slight oak, a velvety palate with caramel, toffee, cacao and whisky, and a sweet, balanced finish with lingering dark cocoa. Shanken News Daily's report on the launch, dated 22 August 2025, says it rolled out on 1 September at $27 a bottle, calls it a limited-edition expression, and lists Apple, Peach, Blackberry, Vanilla and Salted Caramel as the other flavors.

Whether it is a permanent product is therefore unclear. Shanken calls it limited edition, while the brand's own Chocolate page does not label it as such, although it does label Salted Caramel. That conflict is why we do not call it permanent or discontinued. People who search for chocolate Crown Royal near them should check current stock, and a flavored bottle at 35% is a different product from the standard 40% Crown Royal. Shanken also reports that the Blackberry expression reached 610,000 nine-liter cases in its first year, according to Impact Databank.

The Standard Crown Royal Bottles We List

Wikipedia's Crown Royal article says Crown Royal is a blended Canadian whisky owned by Diageo since 2000, made at the Gimli, Manitoba distillery, introduced in 1939 for the royal tour of King George VI. The original comes in a purple felt-like bag with a gold tasseled drawstring, Crown Royal Black is 45% ABV in a black bag, and Crown Royal XO is a blend of 50 whiskies finished in cognac casks, in a grey and gold embroidered bag. It also says Crown Royal XR, a limited numbered release from 2006, is discontinued. So when people ask whether Crown Royal is whiskey, the answer is yes, a Canadian blend.

A Price Warning on the Premium Bottles

US retail listings we found put Crown Royal XO at roughly $34 to $90 for a 750 ml bottle, and the Noble Collection releases at suggested prices of $59.99 to $79.99 when new. Our catalogue lists the XO at $469.99 and a Noble Collection bottle at $699.99, many times higher. We cannot tell from here whether those entries are special editions or listings that need correcting, so we flag the gap plainly: check the exact edition, compare with other sellers, and ask us before ordering any premium Crown Royal bottle.

The question of how much is a bottle of Crown Royal therefore has two answers: about $25 to $35 for the standard and flavored bottles, as Shanken's $27 for Chocolate and our $28.99 Deluxe suggest, and a wide range above that. How to Serve Flavored and Standard Crown Royal

A flavored whisky at 35% is designed for easy drinking, so serve it chilled, over ice or with a mixer, and keep it away from rich food that will clash with the sweetness. The standard Deluxe and Black are more traditional: pour about an ounce, add a drop of water if you like, and taste before you mix. A simple highball of the Deluxe with soda or ginger ale is the easiest way to use it.

If you want to compare, pour half an ounce each of the Deluxe and the Black, which together cost $61.98, and taste them from the lower strength to the higher. The Black is 45%, so you may want a few drops of water. We have not poured these bottles for this guide, so we do not quote our own tasting notes, and the brand's notes for the flavors are the brand's claims.

A few serving ideas help. Chocolate pairs naturally with coffee, vanilla ice cream or hot chocolate, Salted Caramel suits a simple glass over ice, and Blackberry works with soda and a squeeze of lime. For the standard bottles, a highball of the Deluxe with ginger ale is the simplest option, and the Black can be sipped neat. Keep opened bottles sealed and upright, away from heat and light, and remember that sweet flavored whiskies are easy to drink quickly, so pour small measures and serve responsibly. If you are buying as a gift, make sure the recipient is 21 or older, and keep the receipt and order number in case a return is needed within the return window.

Mistakes to Avoid

A few errors come up often with bottles like these:

  • Treating flavored and standard as the same. The flavors are 35% ABV, Deluxe 40% and Black 45%.
  • Assuming a flavor is permanent. Salted Caramel and Blackberry are labelled limited edition, and sources disagree on Chocolate.
  • Paying a premium for a bottle that retails far lower. Check the exact edition and compare prices.
  • Judging by the bag. The bag tells you the line, not the quality.
  • Trusting dated advice. Check the year on any limited release.

One more point is storage. Keep bottles upright, away from heat and light, and reseal them after each pour. If you are buying as a gift, make sure the recipient is 21 or older and enjoys the style, and keep the receipt and order number in case a return is needed within the return window.
