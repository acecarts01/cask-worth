Hibiki 35 Year vs Yamazaki 25 Year: Japan's Greatest Showdown

By Caskworth Editorial July 11, 20268 min readUltra Premium Japanese

Two bottles. Both from Suntory. Both extraordinarily rare. Both priced above $10,000. Hibiki 35 Year and Yamazaki 25 Year represent the absolute pinnacle of Japanese whisky craftsmanship — but which one belongs in your collection?

Hibiki 35 Year — $11,999.99

Hibiki is a blended whisky — an art form the Japanese have elevated beyond what Scotland ever imagined. The 35 Year draws on malt whiskies from Yamazaki and Hakushu distilleries aged a minimum of 35 years, plus grain whisky from Chita. It is matured in five cask types: American white oak, sherry, plum liqueur, Mizunara oak and bourbon. The result is a symphony of sandalwood, ancient dried rose petals, honey and a finish of incomparable complexity.

Yamazaki 25 Year — $4,499.99

Japan's first and most celebrated single malt at 25 years of age. The Mizunara oak influence is profound — incense, coconut and sacred wood notes that exist nowhere else in the whisky world. Complex tropical fruit, spice and a finish that evolves for 40 minutes or more.

Which Should You Buy?

For the blended whisky devotee seeking the absolute pinnacle of the craft — Hibiki 35 Year. For the single malt purist who wants the ultimate expression of Japan's indigenous whisky culture — Yamazaki 25 Year.

Both expressions are available at Caskworth. Hibiki 35 Year and Yamazaki 25 Year ship same-day to 33 U.S. states when you order by 2PM.
