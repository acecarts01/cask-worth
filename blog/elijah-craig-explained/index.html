Elijah Craig 18: Price, Proof and How the Small Batch, Barrel Proof and Rye Compare

The Elijah Craig 18 and the Range From Wikipedia

Wikipedia's Elijah Craig article says it is a bourbon brand made in Kentucky by Heaven Hill and introduced in 1986. The Small Batch is 94 proof, which is 47% ABV, and originally carried a 12 year age statement that Heaven Hill dropped in early 2016 for a blend of 8 and 12 year old bourbon. The Barrel Proof has been released three times a year since 2013, at roughly 125 to 140 proof depending on the batch. The Single Barrel is currently 18 and 23 year, both 90 proof, and the 20, 21 and 22 year bottlings are discontinued.

So a search for Elijah Craig 18 year bourbon whiskey, bourbon Elijah Craig 18 or the Elijah Craig single barrel 18 leads to the 90 proof single barrel. Wikipedia adds that the 18 Year Single Barrel won Best Bourbon at the 2010 San Francisco World Spirits Competition, which is one result from one year. Our Elijah Craig 18 Year Single Barrel is $199.99 and 45%. People ask about the Elijah Craig 18 MSRP, and we could not confirm a current suggested price, so we do not quote one.

Who Was Elijah Craig? A Disputed Claim

The same article says the brand is named for Reverend Elijah Craig, a Baptist preacher, educator and entrepreneur who founded a distillery around 1789 in what became Scott County, Kentucky. It says Heaven Hill credits Craig with inventing true Kentucky bourbon and advertises him as the Father of Bourbon, but that this claim is dubious, citing a 1996 Bourbon Country Reader article. We therefore do not repeat the Father of Bourbon line as fact. The accurate summary is that the brand uses his name, that the invention story is disputed, and that the bottles stand on what is in them.

The older and newer bottles matter more for buyers than the legend. If you want to see how Elijah Craig sits in the wider Heaven Hill family, our Heaven Hill bourbons guide covers the distillery, the 90th Anniversary release and the bonded bottles.

Elijah Craig Small Batch Bourbon: Age Statement and Price

The Elijah Craig Small Batch Bourbon at $34.99 and 47% is the bottle behind searches for Elijah Craig small batch bourbon, and it matches the 94 proof Wikipedia gives. An Elijah Craig small batch review should mention the 2016 change, because older reviews describe a 12 year statement that the current bottle no longer carries. Our catalogue lists it with no age statement. Whether the change matters is a matter of taste, so use older reviews as a guide, not a guarantee.

The Elijah Craig bourbon price in our catalogue is $34.99 for the Small Batch, $44.99 for the Toasted Barrel, $69.99 for the Barrel Proof and $199.99 for the 18 Year Single Barrel. We have no verified detail on the Toasted Barrel beyond the catalogue entry and its 47% strength, so we do not describe it further.

Elijah Craig Barrel Proof and the New Rye

Our Elijah Craig Barrel Proof Bourbon is $69.99 and 66.2%, listed as a 12 year bottle, which falls inside the 125 to 140 proof range Wikipedia gives, since 66.2% is 132.4 proof. Heaven Hill's page for its first Elijah Craig Barrel Proof Rye says it launched on 16 September 2025 as batch A925, 12 years and 3 months old at 108 proof, with a suggested price of $74.99, and that the Barrel Proof Bourbon has the same suggested price. The page gives notes of baking spices, gingerbread and molasses on the nose, stewed fruits and cinnamon on the palate, and a long finish of nutmeg and rye spice.

So does Elijah Craig make a rye whiskey? Yes: the Barrel Proof Rye, with releases planned three times a year in January, May and September. A search for Elijah Craig barrel proof rye leads to it, and we do not currently list it. Our cask strength vs barrel proof guide explains why bottles at this strength taste so different from a 40% pour. For a video, Fred Minnick's Bourbon Review: Elijah Craig Barrel Proof A125 shows a recent Barrel Proof batch.

Which Elijah Craig Should You Buy?

It depends on what you want from the bottle. For everyday drinking and cocktails, the Small Batch at $34.99 is the sensible choice, because at 47% it holds up in an Old Fashioned and costs about $1.40 a pour. For a stronger pour, the Barrel Proof at $69.99 gives far more concentration for about $2.80 a pour, and you can dilute it to taste. For an older age statement, the 18 Year Single Barrel at $199.99 is the step up, and it is softer than the Barrel Proof because it is bottled at 45%.

If you are new to the brand, start with the Small Batch and decide from there. If you already know you like high-proof bourbon, the Barrel Proof is the better value per unit of flavour, and the batch-to-batch variation is part of the appeal. If you want a gift, the 18 Year Single Barrel is the one with an age statement and an award history, although the 2010 award belongs to one particular year of the bottle.

Whichever you choose, check the label for the strength and the batch, keep the bottle upright and sealed away from heat and light, and keep the receipt and order number in case you need to return an unopened bottle within the return window.

How to Compare Elijah Craig Bottles

A flight shows the range. Pour half an ounce each of the Small Batch at 47%, the 18 Year Single Barrel at 45% and the Barrel Proof at 66.2%, which together cost $304.97, into matching glasses, and taste from the lowest strength to the highest. Nose each gently, take a small first sip, and add several drops of water to the Barrel Proof. Write one line per glass about sweetness, spice and finish.

Many drinkers find that the 18 Year is softer despite its age, because it is bottled at only 45%, and that the Barrel Proof is far more intense. The Small Batch sits between them in style. We have not poured these bottles for this guide, so we do not quote our own tasting notes. Keep a glass of water nearby and keep your notes with the bottles so that your next purchase is a choice, not a guess.

Mistakes to Avoid

A few errors come up often with bottles like these:

  • Repeating the Father of Bourbon line. Wikipedia calls the claim dubious.
  • Trusting an old Small Batch review. The 12 year statement was dropped in early 2016.
  • Treating all Barrel Proof batches as equal. Strength varies by batch, so check the label.
  • Comparing the 18 Year with the Barrel Proof by strength. The 18 is 45%, the Barrel Proof about 66%.
  • Skipping provenance on an older bottle. The 20, 21 and 22 year Single Barrels are discontinued.

One more point is storage. Keep bottles upright, away from heat and light, and reseal them after each pour. If you are buying as a gift, make sure the recipient is 21 or older and enjoys the style, and keep the receipt and order number in case a return is needed within the return window.
