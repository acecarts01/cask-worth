Canadian Club Whiskey Price: The History, the Range and the Price Gap to Check

By Caskworth Editorial Published September 6, 2026Updated November 9, 20269 min read

Canadian Club whiskey price depends entirely on which bottle you mean. The standard Canadian Club Classic 12 is listed by US retailers at about $22 to $26, while the five aged and limited bottles in our catalogue run from $479.99 to $1,499.99. Wikipedia says the brand dates to 1858, when Hiram Walker founded his distillery in Detroit before moving it to Windsor, Ontario. This guide gives the documented history, lists the bottles we carry with cost per pour, and flags the price gap so you check the exact edition before you pay.

What Is Canadian Club?

Wikipedia's Canadian Club article says Hiram Walker founded his distillery in 1858 in Detroit, then moved it across the river to Windsor, Ontario, as Prohibition gained momentum in Michigan. The whisky was popular in gentlemen's clubs and became known as Club Whisky, and the word Canadian was added to the label in 1889 and became part of the name in 1890. It also says Al Capone smuggled thousands of cases from Windsor to Detroit during Prohibition. The brand is now made for Suntory Global Spirits according to the infobox, although the history section says it is part of the Jim Beam portfolio, so the article is not consistent.

That is the verified story behind searches for Canadian Club whiskey 1858, the original 1858 bottle and Hiram Walker Canadian Club. It is a blended Canadian whisky, and a search for Canadian Club Canadian whisky or a Canadian Club whisky price leads to the same bottles, so the rules in our Canadian whisky brands guide apply: aged in small wood vessels for at least three years, and at least 40% ABV. For a view of the distillery town, Windsor Star's Inside Hiram Walker and Sons distillery is a useful companion.

The Canadian Club Range

The Wikipedia article lists the current versions as 1858, the base bottle with no age statement at 80 proof, Reserve aged 9 years, Classic aged 12 years, 100% Rye, Apple, Dock No. 57 and a ready-to-drink Mixed & Ready. Discontinued or limited versions include a Limited Edition aged 20 years, the Chronicles series, a 100 Proof aged six years at 50% ABV, a Sherry Cask, and a 30 year old released for the 150th anniversary in 2008. Most versions are 80 proof, which is 40% ABV.

It does not mention a 40 year version or an 8 year version, both of which appear in our catalogue, so we cannot confirm those from the article. Our Canadian Club 40 Year review covers the 40 Year in detail, including the conflicts we found. Searches for a Canadian Club 1960 or a Canadian Club drink lead to older bottles and cocktails that are not covered here.

The Price Gap to Check

US retailers we found list the standard Canadian Club Classic 12 at roughly $22 to $26 for a 750 ml bottle, with reviews noting prices in the $20 to $30 range and a Canadian price near CAD $31.95. Our catalogue lists a Classic 12 Year Limited Edition at $549.99, more than twenty times that. We cannot tell from here whether the catalogue entry is a rare limited edition that justifies the price or a listing that needs correcting, so we flag it plainly.

Before you buy any Canadian Club bottle at a high price, ask for the exact edition, the bottling year and a photo of the label, and compare it with the standard bottle at a shelf price. Review sites that cover the standard Classic 12 give scores in the mid-80s and describe it as good value, which is a different product from a limited edition at $549.99. A Canadian Club whiskey review should always say which bottle was tasted.

The same caution applies to our 100% Rye Aged 8 Year, because the Wikipedia article lists a standard 100% Rye without an 8 year statement. We would rather ask you to check than repeat a claim we cannot support.

Canadian Club Rye and 100% Rye

A search for Canadian Club rye usually means the 100% Rye bottle. Wikipedia lists a 100% Rye among the current versions, and under Canadian law the terms Canadian whisky and rye whisky can refer to the same product even with little rye in it, so a 100% claim is a real distinction. Searches for Canadian Club 100 rye lead to this bottle, and Alberta Premium is another Canadian whisky described as 100% rye.

If you want to compare Canadian Club with another brand, our other Canadian guide lists twelve cheaper bottles by price per pour.

How to Taste and Compare

A fair comparison needs a standard bottle. If you can, taste a standard Classic 12 beside any limited edition, in matching glasses at room temperature. Pour half an ounce of each, nose gently, take a small first sip and hold it on the tongue, and add a drop of water to each. Write a line per glass: sweet or dry, light or full, short or long finish.

Review sites that cover the standard Classic 12 describe a light, easy blend, and they give mid-80s scores, which is a useful baseline. If a limited edition at $549.99 does not taste noticeably different from a $24 bottle to you, you have learned something valuable before spending more. If it does, you have a reason for the price that is your own.

We have not poured the bottles in our catalogue for this guide, so we do not quote our own tasting notes. Serve at room temperature, keep a glass of water nearby, and keep the notes with the bottle so that your next purchase is a choice, not a guess.

Mistakes to Avoid

A few errors come up often with bottles like these:

  • Paying a limited-edition price for a standard bottle. Confirm the edition and year.
  • Trusting an age statement you cannot verify. Ask what the label says.
  • Assuming all Canadian Club is the same. The range runs from a base 1858 bottle to rare aged releases.
  • Skipping the history. Hiram Walker moved from Detroit to Windsor, and the name changed in 1889 to 1890.
  • Using a rare bottle for mixing. A standard bottle does the job for a few dollars.

One more point is storage. Keep bottles upright, away from heat and light, and reseal them after each pour. If you are buying as a gift, make sure the recipient is 21 or older and enjoys the style, and keep the receipt and order number in case a return is needed within the return window.
