Blended Scotch and Blended Malt: The Rules

The Scotch Whisky Association lists five categories on its Scotch whisky categories page. Blended Scotch is a mix of at least one single malt with at least one single grain whisky. A blended malt is a mix of single malts from more than one distillery, with no grain whisky. So a scotch and whiskey blend is a blended Scotch, and an all-malt blend is a different category. Several bottles in this guide are labelled blended malt, and we mark them in the table.

The reason to care is flavour and price. Grain whisky is lighter, so a blend with more grain is usually softer and cheaper, while a blended malt tends to taste fuller. For a video explainer, Good Juice's What is a Blended Whisky? Blends Explained is a handy companion. Our blended Scotch guide goes further into the big three brands.

What Is Johnnie Walker Red?

Wikipedia's Johnnie Walker article says the brand was founded in Kilmarnock, Scotland, in 1820, is owned by Diageo, and that Red Label is a non-age-stated blend, while Black Label is aged 12 years and Double Black, released generally in 2011, adds more peaty malts and heavily charred oak casks. So the Johnnie Walker Red whisky searchers want is the entry bottle, and it is the cheapest of the four at $24.99.

A Johnnie Walker Red vs Black comparison comes down to age and price: Black Label has a 12 year statement and costs $34.99, a $10 premium. The Johnnie Walker Red Label is the better choice for highballs and mixed drinks, and Black Label is the better choice for sipping. Our catalogue lists each at 40%. We have not poured them for this guide, so we do not give tasting notes.

Chivas Brothers Blend vs Chivas Regal 12

Drinks International's launch analysis of The Chivas Brothers Blend says it is no older than the entry-level Chivas Regal 12, but has a higher malt content, which gives it about a 20% higher price. It was a travel retail exclusive at launch. In our catalogue the Chivas Brothers Blend is $39.99 against $29.99 for the Chivas Regal 12, a gap of 33%, so the shelf premium here is larger than the 20% reported at launch.

If you are comparing Chivas Brothers Blend vs Chivas Regal, the practical test is a side-by-side pour at the same strength, since both are listed at 40%. The extra $10 buys a malt-richer blend according to that analysis, and only you can say whether it is worth it.

Which to Buy for Mixing and Which for Sipping

For mixing in a highball or a simple cocktail, the lowest-priced bottles are sensible: Cutty Sark, Ballantine's, Dewar's White Label, The Famous Grouse or Johnnie Walker Red Label. For sipping, the 12 and 15 year bottles are the better choice: Chivas Regal 12, Black Label, and Dewar's 15. For something with more malt in it, the Chivas Brothers Blend is the pick, and for a blended malt the Monkey Shoulder.

A good low-cost experiment is to buy Red Label, Black Label and Chivas Regal 12, which together cost $89.97, and taste them side by side with a few drops of water in each. Write one line per glass and keep the notes.

A Blended Scotch Tasting Flight

The cheapest way to learn what you like is a flight of three. Pour half an ounce each of Johnnie Walker Red Label, Chivas Regal 12 and Chivas Brothers Blend, which together cost $94.97, into matching glasses, and taste them from the lowest price to the highest. Nose each glass gently, take a small first sip, and add a few drops of water to each.

Write one line per glass: sweet or dry, light or full, short or long finish. Many drinkers find that the Red Label is the lightest, that the 12 year adds fruit and softness, and that the Chivas Brothers Blend feels fuller, which fits the higher malt content that Drinks International reports. If you add the Monkey Shoulder blended malt to the flight, taste it last, because a malt-only blend can taste fuller than the grain-softened blends before it.

Keep a glass of water nearby and wait a minute between pours. The point of the flight is not to crown a winner, but to learn which style you prefer, so that your next purchase is a choice and not a guess.

Mistakes to Avoid

A few errors come up often with bottles like these:

  • Assuming blended means poor quality. The word describes how the whisky is made, not how good it is.
  • Confusing a blended malt with a blend. Monkey Shoulder is a blended malt, so check the label category.
  • Paying for age you will not taste. A 12 year blend at $29.99 often beats a no-age bottle at twice the price for sipping.
  • Using your best bottle for mixing. A $19.99 to $24.99 blend does the job in a highball.
  • Comparing across categories. Judge a blend against a blend, not against a single malt.

Whisky does not mature further once bottled, as the Scotch Whisky Association points out, so a bottle kept for years tastes as it did when filled. One more point is storage. Keep bottles upright, away from heat and light, and reseal them after each pour. If you are buying as a gift, make sure the recipient is 21 or older and enjoys the style, and keep the receipt and order number in case a return is needed within the return window.
