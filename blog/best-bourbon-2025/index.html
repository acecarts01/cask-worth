What Is the Best Bourbon? Ten Bottles Compared by Strength, Price and Cost Per Pour

What is the best bourbon? There is no single answer, because published rankings disagree, so this guide compares ten bottles from $25.99 to $499.99 by strength and cost per pour, and tells you which suits which drinker. Bourbon must be made in the United States from at least 51% corn and aged in new charred oak, and everything else is the distiller's choice. Everyday Bourbons: Buffalo Trace, Four Roses and 1792

Buffalo Trace's own Buffalo Trace Bourbon page lists 90 proof, which is 45% ABV, Mash Bill #1, and notes of vanilla, mint and molasses on the nose, a sweet palate of brown sugar and spice giving way to oak, toffee, dark fruit and anise, and a long, smooth finish. Our Buffalo Trace Bourbon is $28.99 and matches that 45%, so it is the benchmark everyday bottle in the table.

Four Roses Small Batch at $32.99 and 45% is a blend of recipes aimed at a lighter, fruitier style, and 1792 Bourbon at $25.99 and 45% is the lowest-priced here, listed in our catalogue as a high-rye bourbon. We have not poured these for this guide, so we do not give our own tasting notes. If you want a first bottle of the best bourbon you can buy easily, either of these costs about $1.04 to $1.32 a pour.

Blanton's, Eagle Rare and the Bottles People Hunt

Buffalo Trace's Blanton’s page calls Blanton's Original Single Barrel the world's first single barrel bourbon. Its product page lists 93 proof (46.5%), Mash Bill #2, no age statement, a nose of nutmeg and spices, dry vanilla with honey, strong caramel and corn on the palate, and a medium finish of corn and nutmeg. Each horse-and-jockey stopper is one of eight, applied at random. Searches for Blanton's single barrel bourbon lead to our Blanton’s Original Single Barrel at $64.99, which matches the 46.5% strength.

The Eagle Rare 10 page lists 90 proof, ten years of ageing, small batches and Mash Bill #1, with toffee, orange peel, herbs, honey, leather and oak on the nose and candied almond and cocoa on the palate. Our Eagle Rare 10 Year is $37.99 and 45%. Note that the distillery says small batches, while our catalogue lists it as a single barrel, so we describe it as small batch. For a spoken view of the Blanton's, SLB Drinks' Is Blanton’s ACTUALLY Worth The Money? is a useful companion.

Weller, Booker's and Stagg: High-Proof Bourbons

Weller bourbon is the wheated family from Buffalo Trace, where wheat replaces rye as the secondary grain. Our catalogue lists Weller Antique 107 at $84.99 and 53.5%, Weller Full Proof at $219.99 and 57%, and William Larue Weller Barrel Proof at $499.99, with a strength conflict noted in our William Larue Weller review. Booker's Bourbon at $104.99 and 60% is a cask strength bottle, and George T. Stagg Barrel Proof at $449.99 and 64.1% is the strongest, so a search for Stagg bourbon lands here.

These are not everyday bottles. At 53% to 64% they are far stronger than a 40% pour, so use a few drops of water and take your time. People who search for Rip Van Winkle bourbon are looking at a different, scarcer tier, and our catalogue lists an Old Rip Van Winkle 10 Year at $995 and the Pappy Van Winkle range above it. Our Pappy Van Winkle 20 Year review covers that tier.

What Is the Difference Between Bourbon and Whiskey?

Bourbon is a type of whiskey. Under 27 CFR 5.143, bourbon must be made in the United States from a mash of at least 51% corn and aged in charred new oak, with distillation and barrel-entry strength limits. Whiskey is the wider category, so every bourbon is a whiskey, but not every whiskey is a bourbon. People ask whether bourbon and whiskey are the same thing, what bourbon whiskey is made from, and what makes bourbon bourbon, and the answer to all three is that rule. Our bourbon vs whiskey guide covers the myths, such as the idea that bourbon must come from Kentucky.

How is bourbon made in practice? Corn, rye or wheat and malted barley are mashed, fermented, distilled and aged in new charred oak barrels, which gives bourbon its sweetness. The secondary grain is the main variable, as in the wheated Weller bottles above. A bourbon barrel is used once for bourbon, which is why used American barrels are common in other whiskies.

A Three-Bottle Bourbon Flight

The surest way to answer the question for yourself is a flight. Pour half an ounce each of Buffalo Trace Bourbon at 45%, Eagle Rare 10 Year at 45% and Weller Antique 107 at 53.5%, which together cost $151.96, into matching tulip glasses, and taste them from the lowest strength to the highest. Nose each glass gently, take a small first sip, and add a few drops of water to the Weller.

Write one line per glass: sweet or dry, soft or spicy, short or long finish. Buffalo Trace and Eagle Rare share a mash bill according to the distillery, so the difference you notice is mostly age and selection, while the Weller shows what a wheated mash bill and higher strength add. That is a better guide to what you like than any ranking.

If you want a fourth glass, add Blanton's Original Single Barrel at 46.5%. Keep a glass of water nearby, wait a minute between pours, and keep your notes with the bottles so that your next purchase is a choice, not a guess.

Mistakes to Avoid

A few errors come up often with bottles like these:

  • Chasing allocated bottles first. Learn the style with a $28.99 bottle before paying for a rare one.
  • Trusting a single ranking. Lists disagree, so check a few and compare prices.
  • Ignoring strength. A 60% bottle tastes bolder than a 45% one, whatever the grain.
  • Assuming bourbon must be from Kentucky. The rule is United States, not Kentucky.
  • Over-diluting. A few drops of water are plenty.

One more point is storage. Keep bottles upright, away from heat and light, and reseal them after each pour. If you are buying as a gift, make sure the recipient is 21 or older and enjoys the style, and keep the receipt and order number in case a return is needed within the return window.

  4. You want to try wheated bourbon: Weller Antique 107 at $84.99.
  5. You want maximum strength: Booker’s at $104.99 or George T. Stagg Barrel Proof at $449.99.
