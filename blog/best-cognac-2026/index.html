What VS, VSOP and XO Mean

The letters on a cognac label are not marketing. The Cognac trade body, the BNIC, sets them out: VS means at least 2 years of ageing, VSOP at least 4 years and XO at least 10 years, and the age of a cognac is that of the youngest eau-de-vie in the blend. The BNIC also says Cognac cannot be sold before a minimum of two years of ageing, without interruption and exclusively in oak vessels. The rules are on the BNIC’s protected origin page.

These are minimums, so a good house usually blends older spirits than the label requires. That is why two VSOPs at the same price can taste different, and why the grade tells you the floor rather than the ceiling. For a visual explanation of the grades and the big houses, Julien Miquel’s What is VSOP Cognac? video compares Hennessy, Courvoisier, Martell and Rémy Martin. It is an independent educator’s explainer, so use it for context and our listings for prices.

Serving matters as much as the grade. Pour a small measure, about an ounce, and let it sit for a minute so the aroma opens. Add a few drops of water or a single ice cube if you find the spirit sharp, and keep the bottle upright and away from heat and light. A VS that you enjoy in a cocktail and an XO that you savour neat are a better pair than two XOs, because they give you a use for each. Opened bottles keep well for months if the closure is tight, so there is no pressure to finish a bottle quickly.

For a rich, French-region view of the best cognacs, buy one small house and one big house at the same grade and taste them side by side. The differences are the point of the exercise.

What Makes It French Cognac

Every bottle in this guide is French cognac, which means it is made in a delimited area in western France under strict rules. The BNIC says cognac is made in a delimited geographical area, that the vineyards are divided into six crus, and that Fine Champagne is a blend of eaux-de-vie from Grande Champagne, with at least 50% from Grande Champagne, and Petite Champagne. Those details are on the BNIC’s French-language appellation guide.

People who search for a cognac house france are usually asking which names are worth knowing, and the answer ranges from a large company to a small family estate. The label rules apply to both. That is why a $22.99 bottle and a $199.99 bottle can both be legally cognac: the minimum rules are the same, and the difference is the age, the blend and the house.

If you want to read more about what separates cognac from other brandies, our guide to cognac vs brandy explains the rules in detail.

How to Choose

Use this short path:

  1. Decide how you will drink it. Mixed drinks need VS. Sipping suits VSOP or XO.
  2. Set a budget. VS under $40, VSOP under $60, XO from $150.
  3. Pick a house. Choose a big name if you want the familiar style, or a smaller house if you want to explore.
  4. Check the strength. Every bottle in this guide is 40% ABV.
  5. Use the right glass. A tulip-shaped glass shows the aroma better than a wide snifter, and a good glass improves any grade.
