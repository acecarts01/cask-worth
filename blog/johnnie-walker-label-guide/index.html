Johnnie Walker Labels in Order

People search for johnnie walker labels in order, the johnnie walker colours in order and the johnnie walker label ranking, and the clean answer is that the colours roughly follow price and prestige, not a single scale of age. In our catalog, the order from the entry level up runs: Red, Black, Double Black, Green, Gold Reserve, Platinum or Aged 18, then Blue and the Blue-label special editions, with The John Walker at the top.

Three things to notice about the johnnie walker color order:

  • Age statements appear on only some labels. Black (12), Green (15), Platinum (18), Aged 18 and XR (21) carry one. Red, Double Black, Gold Reserve and Blue do not.
  • Price does not rise strictly with age. The Green Label 15 Year at $49.99 costs less than the no-age-statement Gold Label Reserve at $54.99.
  • The Blue tier has its own sub-ladder. Our catalog lists Blue Label at $199.99, with special editions between $229.99 and $349.99, plus King George V at $249.99 and The John Walker at $599.99.

If you want a shortcut, think of the johnnie walker label tiers as three bands: everyday (Red, Black, Double Black), step-up (Green, Gold, Swing, the 18 year olds) and prestige (XR 21 and the Blue tier). Our broader guide to the best Scotch whisky to buy puts these bottles beside single malts at the same prices.

Blended Scotch vs Blended Malt: What the Labels Are

Johnnie Walker is a blending house, so every bottle is a blend, but the type of blend differs. The Scotch Whisky Association defines Blended Scotch Whisky as one or more Single Malt Scotch Whiskies blended with one or more Single Grain whiskies, and Blended Malt Scotch Whisky as a blend of two or more Single Malt Scotch Whiskies. The full definitions are on the Scotch Whisky Association categories page.

In our listings, almost every Johnnie Walker is classed as Blended Scotch. The exception is Green Label 15 Year, which our catalog lists as a Blended Malt Scotch, meaning it is made only from malt whiskies. That one difference explains why Green Label is often described as the closest label to a single malt in feel. It also costs $49.99, between Black and Gold, so it is a sensible bridge if you are moving from blends toward malts.

The age on a Johnnie Walker label tells you about the blend, not one cask. Johnnie Walker says its Aged 18 Years draws on whiskies from Blair Athol in the Highlands, Glen Elgin in Speyside and Cardhu, and says the creaminess of Cardhu flows into a long, sumptuous finish. See the details on the official Johnnie Walker Aged 18 Years page, which also lists it at 40% ABV.

Red Label vs Black Label: Is the Step Worth $10?

The johnnie walker red label price is $24.99, and Black Label is $34.99. The difference is $10 a bottle, or about 40 cents more per ounce, and it buys you the first age statement in the range, 12 years. That makes Black Label an easy recommendation if you will drink it neat or with a little ice, because you get more age for a very small extra cost.

Red Label still has a role. If you are mixing with cola, soda or ginger ale, most of the character of the whisky is diluted, and the lower price per drink makes sense. In practice:

  • Highballs and mixed drinks: Red Label, because the mixer does most of the work.
  • Neat, with ice or with water: Black Label, because the 12 year age statement is worth a few dollars.
  • Gifting to someone who knows Johnnie Walker: Black Label or higher, since Red can look like the default choice.

If you want to see how Black Label stacks up against single malts in the same range, our guide to the best Scotch under $100 compares them directly.

Green Label and Gold Label Reserve

Two labels sit between Black and the 18 year olds, and buyers often ask which is better value. The johnnie walker green label price is $49.99, and the johnnie walker gold label reserve price is $54.99. On paper the Green Label 15 Year is the better value: it carries a 15 year age statement, a 43% strength and a malt-only blend, all for $5 less. The Green Label 15 Year is the one to buy if you want age and strength for the money.

Gold Label Reserve has no age statement and is bottled at 40%. That does not make it a worse whisky, since age statements are only one measure, but it does make it harder to judge on paper. Use this simple rule: if you value a stated age and a higher strength, choose Green. If you prefer to buy on style and presentation, try the Gold Label Reserve at $54.99.

Johnnie Walker also lists Double Black at $39.99 and Swing at $59.99, both without age statements. Swing is bottled at 43%, which is three points above most of the range. If you want a higher strength without moving up in age, Swing is the cheaper way to get it, though it costs $10 more than Green Label.

Blue Label Johnnie Walker Price and the Blue Tier

The blue label johnnie walker price is $199.99 at Caskworth, which is nearly six times the Black Label. On the official page, Johnnie Walker says only one in 10,000 casks is hand-selected to craft Blue Label, including casks from long-closed ghost distilleries, and lists the US bottling at 40% ABV. You can read that on the official Johnnie Walker Blue Label page. Our listing for the Johnnie Walker Blue Label gives the current price and strength.

Which Johnnie Walker Label Should You Buy?

Match the label to the occasion:

  • Cocktails and highballs: Red Label at $24.99.
  • Your first proper Johnnie Walker: Black Label 12 Year at $34.99.
  • A step up in strength and malt character: Green Label 15 Year at $49.99.
  • A recognisable gift: Platinum or Aged 18 at $79.99 to $89.99, or Blue Label at $199.99.
  • A milestone gift: XR 21 Year at $149.99 for an age statement, or Blue Label and above for the name.

A useful way to decide is cost per pour. Black Label works out to about $1.40 an ounce and Blue Label to about $8.00. If you drink two ounces a week, the Black Label bottle lasts about three months and the Blue Label bottle costs about $16 a week. Knowing that, it is easier to decide how often you want to open each bottle and which one belongs on your shelf.
