What Eagle Rare 10 Year Is

Buffalo Trace Distillery’s Eagle Rare Aged 10 Years page lists it as a Kentucky straight bourbon whiskey at 90 proof (45% ABV), aged 10 years, made with Mash Bill #1. The brand page adds that every barrel is selected to offer consistent flavour with a seemingly individual personality, and says the bourbon is aged for no less than ten years.

One detail to check: our catalogue lists the bottle as a single barrel, while Buffalo Trace’s product page describes it as small-batch. Because the producer’s own wording matters, read the label of the bottle you receive for the exact description, and do not assume a particular barrel or batch. The strength, age and style (Kentucky straight bourbon) are the facts that match across both.

The federal rules set the floor for any straight bourbon: made from at least 51% corn, aged in charred new oak and not less than two years, as set out in 27 CFR 5.143. Eagle Rare goes well beyond the two-year minimum with its 10 year age statement.

Tasting Notes From Buffalo Trace

Buffalo Trace gives full notes. The nose is described as complex, with aromas of toffee, hints of orange peel, herbs, honey, leather and oak. The taste is bold, dry and delicate with notes of candied almonds and very rich cocoa. The finish is dry and lingering. The distillery suggests serving it neat, on the rocks or in spirit-forward cocktails.

What that means in practice:

  • Dry, not sweet. The words dry and delicate, with cocoa and almonds, point to a drier style than a caramel-forward bourbon.
  • Herbal and orange notes give it a lifted, savoury edge.
  • A long dry finish, which makes it a good neat pour.

The page also lists several medals, including a 2025 gold at the International Wine & Spirits Competition. Treat medals as a single signal, not a guarantee that you will like it. For a second opinion, SLB Drinks’ video Is Eagle Rare Worth It?! gives an independent view.

  • Buffalo Trace Bourbon ($28.99): the cheapest and the most widely available of the three. A good everyday bourbon and a fair baseline.

Eagle Rare Proof and Mash Bill

Two details come up often. The first is proof: Buffalo Trace lists the bourbon at 90 proof, and proof is twice the ABV, so 90 proof is 45% alcohol by volume. That is above the 40% federal minimum for bottling and in the middle of the range for popular bourbons.

The second is the mash bill. Buffalo Trace lists Eagle Rare as made with Mash Bill #1, and its page does not give grain percentages, so any figure you see elsewhere is not the distillery’s. What the federal rules tell you is the floor: a bourbon mash is at least 51% corn. If you want to compare mash bills across brands, read each producer’s own page and treat unsourced percentages with caution.

Together, the age, the proof and the mash bill tell you what you are buying. An age statement of 10 years, a strength of 45% and a drier, herbal profile is the answer to the question of what Eagle Rare is, and it is why the bottle has the following it does.

A Side-by-Side Tasting Plan

The best way to decide whether Eagle Rare is worth it is to taste it against its neighbours. Buy Buffalo Trace Bourbon ($28.99), Eagle Rare 10 Year ($37.99) and Blanton’s Original Single Barrel ($64.99), which together cost $131.97. Pour about half an ounce of each into matching glasses, smell them, taste them neat in price order and then add a few drops of water and taste again.

Write down what you notice: sweetness, dryness, herbal notes, heat and finish. Many people find the difference between the first two is mainly dryness and age, and the difference to the third is strength and body. If you cannot tell them apart, buy the cheapest and keep the difference. If you can, you have learned which style you want to buy again, which is worth more than any ranking. Common mistakes to avoid are tasting in the wrong order, using a tiny glass that hides the aroma and judging a bottle after a single sip.

How to Serve It

Buffalo Trace suggests neat, on the rocks or in spirit-forward cocktails, and the featured cocktail on its page is the Eagle Rare Whiskey Sour. At 45% ABV it handles all three. For a first taste, pour a small measure neat in a tulip glass, let it sit for a minute and sip. Add a few drops of water if you want to open the aroma. If you prefer ice, one large cube melts slowly and keeps the dryness without watering it down quickly.

Store the bottle upright, away from heat and sunlight. Bourbon does not improve in the bottle, so there is no reason to wait to open it. Once it is open, keep the cap tight and finish the bottle within a reasonable time so the aroma stays fresh, and share a pour with a friend, because a bourbon like this is more fun to compare with someone else.
