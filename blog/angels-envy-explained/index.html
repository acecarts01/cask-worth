Angel's Envy Cask Strength: The 2025 Release

The brand's Angel's Envy 10 Cask Strength page lists the 2025 release as aged a cumulative 10 years in new American white oak and port wine barrels, with a port finish after the oak ageing. It is bottled at 61.3% ABV, 122.6 proof, without proofing down, and it is the 14th edition in the brand's Cask Strength Archives. The page gives these tasting notes: sweet cinnamon toast, creamy fruit nougat and candied nuts on the nose, baklava, ripe apricot, creme brulee and cognac with a touch of grains of paradise on the palate, and rum raisin cake on the finish.

The page does not state a mash bill, a price or a release date beyond the year. Retailer listings show other strengths for the same bottle, such as 59%, and an annual release can differ from year to year, so the label on the bottle is the authority. Our catalogue lists the Angel's Envy Cask Strength Port Finish at 60% ABV and $249.99, close to the brand's figure for 2025 but not identical, so ask us which year a bottle is before you order.

For a spoken companion, Bourbon Pursuit's Best Angel's Envy Cask Strength Yet? | Whiskey Quickie reviews a recent edition. Our cask strength vs barrel proof guide explains why a cask strength bottle is so much stronger than a standard 40% pour.

Angel's Envy Bourbon Whiskey and the Core Range

People who search for Angel's Envy bourbon whiskey usually mean the original port-finished bourbon. Our catalogue lists the Angel's Envy Kentucky Straight Bourbon at $44.99 and 43.3%, finished in ruby port barrels, and the Angel's Envy Finished in Tawny Port Barrels at $54.99 and 43.3%, finished in tawny port casks.

The Angel's Envy Rye Whiskey at $79.99 and 50% is the Angel's Envy finished rye, finished in Caribbean rum barrels according to our catalogue, and searches for Angel's Envy rye lead here. The brand's press release for Triple Oak says the port-finished bourbon launched in 2011 and the Caribbean rum cask rye was announced in 2013, and that the brand was founded in 2010.

A short Angel's Envy bourbon review would say that the base bourbon is the right place to start, because it shows the port finish at a standard strength and a price under $50. We have not poured these bottles for this guide, so we do not quote our own tasting notes. Our finished bourbon guide compares other finished bottles.

What Is Angel's Envy Triple Oak?

Angel's Envy Triple Oak is a newer release that we do not currently list. The brand's press release, dated 13 September 2024, says it is a blend of whiskeys finished in three oak barrel types: Hungarian oak from Hungary, Chinkapin oak from Kentucky and French oak from France. It is 92 proof, or 46% ABV, with a suggested price of $74.99, sold at the Louisville distillery from 14 September 2024 and nationally from 1 October 2024. The release says it is the first addition to the Signature Series in over a decade.

Press release tasting notes put French oak as the strongest flavour, with baking spices, chocolate and cinnamon, Chinkapin oak with caramel, vanilla and coffee, and Hungarian oak adding depth. Those are the brand's own claims, not independent findings. Buyers searching for Angel's Envy triple oaked will find this bottle, and the Triple Oak name is the one to use on the label.

Angel's Envy Bottled in Bond

Breaking Bourbon's review of the bottle, Angel's Envy Bottled in Bond Cask Strength Bourbon, says it was first released in June 2024 as a 375 ml distillery exclusive, with 750 ml bottles sold nationwide from late 2025. It lists 100 proof, no age statement although the company says nearly six years, barrels filled in spring 2018, no finish, and a $96 suggested price. It meets the bottled in bond requirement, which requires one distilling season, one distillery and 100 proof.

That makes the Angel's Envy bottled in bond a very different bottle from the port-finished bourbons: unfinished, at 50%, and from one distilling season. We do not currently list it. If you want it, check the label and the year before you pay.

Who Makes and Owns Angel's Envy

The Triple Oak press release says the distillery is at 500 East Main Street in Louisville, that it opened in 2016 as the first full-production whiskey distillery in downtown Louisville, and that an $8.2 million expansion in June 2022 doubled annual guest capacity. It says the brand is produced by Louisville Distilling Company, a subsidiary of Bacardi Limited, which answers the question of who makes Angel's Envy and who owns it. The release names Owen Martin as master distiller.

How to Taste a Port-Finished Bourbon

A port finish adds dark fruit and sweetness, so give the whiskey time. Pour about an ounce of the standard bourbon into a tulip glass and let it rest for a minute. Nose it gently, and look for the dark fruit, vanilla and caramel that the brand and the catalogue describe. Take a small first sip, hold it on the tongue, and notice whether the port arrives before the oak.

The Cask Strength needs more care. At 60% or more, pour half an ounce, let it sit for several minutes, and add two or three drops of water before the second sip. The brand’s notes for the 2025 edition suggest cinnamon toast, nougat, apricot and rum raisin, which a drop of water often brings forward. Do not judge on the first sip, which is mostly heat.

The best comparison is to taste the $44.99 base bourbon and the Cask Strength side by side, in matching glasses, and write a line for each. You will see how strength changes the same idea. Serve at room temperature, keep water nearby, and keep your notes with the bottle.

Mistakes to Avoid

A few errors come up often with finished bourbons:

  • Assuming every Angel’s Envy is port finished. The rye is rum finished and the Bottled in Bond is unfinished.
  • Trusting one strength figure. Cask Strength changes by year, so check the label.
  • Paying a resale premium without checking the suggested price. The brand’s own pages give $74.99 for Triple Oak and $96 for Bottled in Bond.
  • Confusing Triple Oak with Triple Oaked searches. They lead to the same bottle, so use the label name.
  • Over-diluting. A few drops of water are plenty.

One more point is storage. Keep bottles upright, away from heat and light, and reseal them after each pour. If you are buying as a gift, the base bourbon at $44.99 is a safe choice for someone who likes sweeter whiskey, and you should make sure the recipient is 21 or older and enjoys bourbon.
