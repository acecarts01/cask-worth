Michter’s Bourbon Guide: Why It Is the World’s Most Admired Whiskey in 2026

By Caskworth Editorial October 16, 20268 min readMarket News

Michter’s has been named the number one whiskey on Drinks International’s World’s Most Admired Whiskies list for the fourth year in a row, according to the 2026 ranking announced on August 25. The Louisville-based brand makes bourbon, rye and American whiskey, and its range runs from the everyday US*1 bottles to rare 20 and 25 year releases.

Michter’s Named World’s Most Admired Whiskey for 2026

Drinks International published its 2026 World’s Most Admired Whiskies list, a top-50 ranking, in late August, and Michter’s took first place for the fourth consecutive year. The ranking is determined by an academy of independent drinks buyers, journalists, bartenders and whisky experts from more than 25 countries. Drinks International editor Shay Waterworth said that “Michter’s continues to raise the bar each year.”

It is worth knowing where the announcement came from. The details above come from a press release issued by Michter’s and from trade coverage by The Whiskey Wash, which reported the news on August 26. The full top-50 list, including which brands placed behind Michter’s, is published by Drinks International.

Where Michter’s Is Made

Michter’s is based in Louisville, Kentucky. The company describes three main sites:

  • Shively facility, Louisville. Its main distillery.
  • Fort Nelson Distillery, downtown Louisville. A second distillery in the historic Fort Nelson Building. It uses a pot still system that came from Michter’s Pennsylvania operation and offers tours and tastings.
  • Springfield, Kentucky. A 205-acre farm where the company grows estate grain.

Tasting US*1 Bourbon and the 10 Year Side by Side

If you want to understand how Michter’s changes with age, pour the two bourbons next to each other:

  1. Start with US*1 Bourbon. The product page lists notes of caramel corn, raisin and oak. Take a small sip neat and notice its sweetness and warmth.
  2. Move to the 10 Year. Expect more depth: caramel, praline and warming spice, with a longer finish.
  3. Add a few drops of water to each and taste again to see which one opens up more.

This is the simplest way to decide whether an older bottle is worth the step up for you. Our guide to how to drink bourbon explains the technique.

A Michter’s Rye Manhattan

Michter’s US*1 Rye makes a rounded Manhattan:

Ingredients: 2 oz Michter’s US*1 Rye, 1 oz sweet vermouth, 2 dashes Angostura bitters, a cocktail cherry.
Method: Stir with ice for about 30 seconds, strain into a chilled coupe and garnish with the cherry.

Michter’s Beyond Bourbon: Rye and American Whiskey

According to Michter’s, its portfolio covers bourbon, rye, sour mash whiskey and American whiskey, with limited-production releases aged to peak maturity. At Caskworth, the rye side runs from the everyday Michter’s US*1 Rye to the rare Michter’s 25 Year American Rye Pre-Prohibition Era, which its product page describes as coming from Michter’s original Pennsylvania distillery operation. Rye fans who want to compare more brands can read our rye whiskey showdown.

Which Michter’s Should You Buy?

  • Trying Michter’s for the first time: start with US*1 Bourbon. It shows the house style at an approachable price.
  • A rye fan: US*1 Rye is a good base for stirred cocktails. Our best rye whiskey guide compares more options.
  • Sipping something special: the 10 Year is a clear step up in depth and warmth.
  • Collecting or gifting: the 20 and 25 Year bottles are released in limited quantities and are bottled at full strength, so serve them neat with a little water. Our guide to allocated bourbon explains why bottles like these are hard to find.

Why the Oldest Michter’s Bottles Cost So Much

The 20 and 25 Year Michter’s bourbons are priced very differently from the US*1 bottles, and the reasons are straightforward:

  • Time. Every year in the barrel loses some whiskey to evaporation, so an old bourbon is made from far fewer bottles than were first filled.
  • Selection. The 20 Year is released only when the master distiller decides it has reached its peak, so supply is limited and unpredictable.
  • Demand. Collectors and gift buyers compete for a small number of bottles, which pushes prices up. Our rare whisky investment update looks at how older bourbon behaves as a collectable.

How to Serve Michter’s

  • US*1 Bourbon: neat or with one large ice cube. It also works well in an Old Fashioned.
  • US*1 Rye: in a Manhattan, or neat for a spicier sip.
  • 10 Year: neat in a tulip glass, with a few drops of water if you like.
  • 20 and 25 Year: neat, in small pours, with water added gradually, because they are bottled at over 57% ABV.

Our full how to drink bourbon guide explains each method.

What the Ranking Does and Does Not Tell You

A “most admired” ranking reflects how respected a brand is among people who work with whiskey every day. That is a useful signal, particularly when it is consistent for four years in a row. It does not tell you which individual bottle you will like best. Michter’s makes everything from an everyday bottle to a very expensive 25 year release, and your own taste, budget and the occasion matter more than a ranking. If you like to follow trade news before buying, our guides to the 2026 bourbon glut and bourbons under $75 give useful market context.

The simplest way to test the ranking for yourself is to taste the US*1 Bourbon first, then decide whether the 10 Year is worth stepping up to. That small experiment tells you more about your own palate than any list can.

What Else Is Happening in Bourbon This Year

Michter’s fourth win arrives during an unusual year for American whiskey. Record warehouse inventories have changed pricing and production plans across Kentucky, which we explain in our bourbon glut guide. At the same time, collectors continue to chase allocated bottles, as covered in what makes bourbon allocated.
